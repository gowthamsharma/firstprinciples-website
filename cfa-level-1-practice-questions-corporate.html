CFA Level I practice questions: Corporate issuers, with worked answers

By Gowtham KumarPractice questions

Ten original corporate issuers questions written like the real Level I exam: short cases with data, three options each, and worked explanations. Capital budgeting, the cost of capital, leverage, working capital and governance. Corporate issuers carries 6–9% of the exam from 2027.

Corporate issuers (formerly corporate finance) asks how companies choose investments, how they are financed and governed, and how they manage working capital. The calculations are capital budgeting and the cost of capital; the concepts are governance and stakeholder conflicts.

Topic weight from 2027: 6–9% of Level I, about 11 to 16 of the 180 questions.

10 questions · Level I format · about 15 to 20 minutes

Choose an answer to see the explanation.

  1. Tierra Agro must choose between two mutually exclusive irrigation projects. Each costs $500,000, and its cost of capital is 10%. Expected cash flows ($ thousands) are:

    YearProject AProject B
    1150300
    2200200
    3250100
    410050

    The IRR of Project A is 15.3% and that of Project B is 15.9%. Tierra should most likely choose:

    Answer: B.

    NPV at 10%: A=−500+1501.1+2001.12+2501.13+1001.14=57.8A = -500 + \frac{150}{1.1} + \frac{200}{1.1^2} + \frac{250}{1.1^3} + \frac{100}{1.1^4} = 57.8A=−500+1.1150​+1.12200​+1.13250​+1.14100​=57.8. B=−500+3001.1+2001.12+1001.13+501.14=47.3B = -500 + \frac{300}{1.1} + \frac{200}{1.1^2} + \frac{100}{1.1^3} + \frac{50}{1.1^4} = 47.3B=−500+1.1300​+1.12200​+1.13100​+1.1450​=47.3.

    For mutually exclusive projects, choose the higher NPV, because NPV measures the value added at the company's actual cost of capital. The rankings differ because B returns cash sooner: IRR implicitly assumes reinvestment at the IRR itself, which favours early cash flows. The choice should not be made on IRR when the two measures conflict.

  2. Helios Chemicals has equity with a market value of €600 million and a book value of €400 million. Its bonds have a market value of €400 million and a book value of €500 million, and they yield 5.5% to maturity. The company's cost of equity is 11%, and its marginal tax rate is 25%. Helios's target capital structure matches its current market-value weights.

    Helios's weighted average cost of capital is closest to:

    Answer: C.

    Use market-value (or target) weights: equity 600 / 1,000 = 60%, debt 400 / 1,000 = 40%. The after-tax cost of debt = 5.5% × (1 − 0.25) = 4.125%. WACC=0.60×11%+0.40×4.125%=6.60%+1.65%=8.25%WACC = 0.60 \times 11\% + 0.40 \times 4.125\% = 6.60\% + 1.65\% = 8.25\%WACC=0.60×11%+0.40×4.125%=6.60%+1.65%=8.25%.

    8.80% uses the pre-tax cost of debt. 7.18% uses book-value weights (equity 4/94/94/9, debt 5/95/95/9), which do not reflect what investors would pay for the company's securities today.

  3. An analyst estimates the cost of equity for Baltic Shipping using the capital asset pricing model. The yield on long-term government bonds is 3.5%, Baltic's equity beta is 1.2, and she uses an equity risk premium of 5.5%.

    Baltic's cost of equity is closest to:

    Answer: A.

    CAPM: re=Rf+β×ERP=3.5%+1.2×5.5%=3.5%+6.6%=10.1%r_e = R_f + \beta \times ERP = 3.5\% + 1.2 \times 5.5\% = 3.5\% + 6.6\% = 10.1\%re​=Rf​+β×ERP=3.5%+1.2×5.5%=3.5%+6.6%=10.1%.

    9.0% ignores beta (3.5% + 5.5%). 10.8% multiplies the beta by the whole of the risk-free rate plus the premium: 1.2×(3.5%+5.5%)1.2 \times (3.5\% + 5.5\%)1.2×(3.5%+5.5%).

  4. Pico Sports sells 100,000 cycling helmets a year at $20 each. Variable costs are $12 per unit and fixed operating costs are $400,000 a year.

    If unit sales rise by 10%, Pico's operating income will most likely rise by:

    Answer: C.

    Degree of operating leverage DOL=Q(P−V)Q(P−V)−F=100,000×8800,000−400,000=2.0DOL = \dfrac{Q(P - V)}{Q(P - V) - F} = \dfrac{100{,}000 \times 8}{800{,}000 - 400{,}000} = 2.0DOL=Q(P−V)−FQ(P−V)​=800,000−400,000100,000×8​=2.0. A 10% rise in sales gives about 2.0 × 10% = 20% more operating income.

    Check: at 110,000 units, operating income = 880,000−400,000=480,000880{,}000 - 400{,}000 = 480{,}000880,000−400,000=480,000, which is 20% above 400,000. 10% would apply only with no fixed costs, and 40% doubles the DOL.

  5. An analyst gathers the following annual data for Nordwind Tools (in € millions):

    Revenue730.0
    Cost of goods sold547.5
    Accounts receivable90.0
    Inventory90.0
    Accounts payable57.0

    Using a 365-day year, Nordwind's cash conversion cycle is closest to:

    Answer: A.

    Days sales outstanding = 90/(730/365)=4590 / (730 / 365) = 4590/(730/365)=45 days. Days of inventory on hand = 90/(547.5/365)=6090 / (547.5 / 365) = 6090/(547.5/365)=60 days. Days payables outstanding = 57/(547.5/365)=3857 / (547.5 / 365) = 3857/(547.5/365)=38 days. Cash conversion cycle = 45+60−38=6745 + 60 - 38 = 6745+60−38=67 days.

    105 days leaves out payables. 143 days adds payables instead of subtracting them. Supplier credit shortens the time that the company's own cash is tied up.

  6. A supplier offers Kumasi Textiles trade credit on terms of '2/102/102/10, net 40': a 2% discount if Kumasi pays within 10 days, otherwise full payment is due in 40 days. Kumasi's bank would lend it money at 12% a year.

    The effective annual cost of not taking the discount, and the better choice for Kumasi, are closest to:

    Answer: B.

    Forgoing the discount means paying 100 instead of 98 in exchange for 30 more days of credit. Cost =(1+298)365/30−1=27.9%= \left(1 + \dfrac{2}{98}\right)^{365/30} - 1 = 27.9\%=(1+982​)365/30−1=27.9% a year. That is far above the 12% bank rate, so Kumasi should borrow from the bank to pay early.

    24.8% is the simple, uncompounded annual rate (2/98×365/30)(2/98 \times 365/30)(2/98×365/30). 2.0% is the cost for 30 days, not a year.

  7. At the annual meeting of Delta Gold Mines, shareholders vote on three governance proposals:

    1. Increase the share of independent directors on the board from 40% to 60%.

    2. Combine the roles of chief executive and board chair, and elect directors in staggered classes, one-third each year.

    3. Introduce an annual advisory shareholder vote on executive pay.

    Which proposal is most likely to weaken the board's accountability to shareholders?

    Answer: B.

    Combining the CEO and chair roles reduces the board's independent oversight of management. Staggered elections mean shareholders cannot replace a majority of the board in a single year, which entrenches the board and can deter takeovers. Both weaken accountability.

    More independent directors and a say-on-pay vote both strengthen shareholders' ability to monitor management.

  8. Ridgeway Retail is highly leveraged and close to breaching its loan covenants. Its management, who own large share options, propose spending most of the company's remaining cash on a high-risk expansion. The expansion would multiply the share price if it succeeds and would probably bankrupt the company if it fails.

    The proposal most likely reflects a conflict of interest between:

    Answer: A.

    When a company is near distress, shareholders (and managers holding options) keep all of the upside of a risky bet, while losses beyond the equity fall on creditors. Taking extra risk therefore transfers value from creditors to shareholders. This is the classic shareholder–creditor conflict, which covenants are designed to limit.

    Neither customers nor regulators are the party whose capital is put at risk here.

  9. An unlevered company is valued at $500 million. It plans to issue $200 million of perpetual debt and use the proceeds to buy back shares. Its corporate tax rate is 25%. Assume the Modigliani–Miller conditions with corporate taxes and no costs of financial distress.

    After the recapitalisation, the value of the company is closest to:

    Answer: A.

    With corporate taxes, MM Proposition I says VL=VU+tDV_L = V_U + tDVL​=VU​+tD, because interest is tax-deductible and the tax shield has a present value of tDtDtD for perpetual debt: 500+0.25×200500 + 0.25 \times 200500+0.25×200 = $550 million.

    $500 million is the result without taxes, where capital structure does not matter. $700 million adds the whole of the debt rather than only the tax shield.

  10. The treasurer of Coastline Hotels lists ways the company could meet an unexpected $30 million payment next month:

    1. Use cash already held in its operating accounts.

    2. Draw on an existing, committed bank credit line.

    3. Sell one of its hotels.

    Which of these is a secondary source of liquidity?

    Answer: C.

    Primary sources of liquidity are those a company uses in its normal course of business: cash balances, short-term funds, committed credit lines and cash flow from operations. Secondary sources, such as selling long-term assets, renegotiating debt or filing for bankruptcy protection, change the company's financial or operating position. They are usually a sign of financial stress.

    Selling a hotel shrinks the business, so it is a secondary source.

Three things to remember for corporate issuers

  • NPV beats IRR whenever the two disagree on mutually exclusive projects.
  • Use the after-tax cost of debt in the WACC.
  • The cash conversion cycle adds inventory and receivable days and subtracts payable days.

How to use these questions

Answer each question before you look at the explanation, and time yourself: the real exam allows about 90 seconds per question. Mark every question you got right for the wrong reason as wrong. When you miss one, write down the idea behind it rather than the answer, and come back to the page in a week.

These questions are written by First Principles to match the style and level of the exam. They are not taken from CFA Institute's curriculum or mock exams, which you should also use: they are included with your exam registration.

Questions people ask

Are these real CFA exam questions?

No. CFA Institute does not publish past exam questions. These are original questions written to match the Level I format: a short case, often with a table of data, followed by three answer options. Each explanation shows the working and why the two wrong options are wrong.

How many corporate issuers questions are on Level I?

Corporate issuers carries 6–9% of the exam from 2027, which is roughly 11 to 16 of the 180 questions.

Where can I find more Level I practice questions?

Use the practice questions and mock exams in CFA Institute's learning ecosystem first; they come with registration. Then use one third-party question bank, and redo every question you missed.
