CFA Level I practice questions: Corporate issuers, with worked answers

By Gowtham KumarPractice questions

Ten original corporate issuers questions in the Level I format, three options each, with an explanation for every answer. Capital budgeting, the cost of capital, leverage, working capital and governance. Corporate issuers carries 6–9% of the exam from 2027.

Corporate issuers (formerly corporate finance) asks how companies choose investments, how they are financed and governed, and how they manage working capital. The calculations are capital budgeting and the cost of capital; the concepts are governance and stakeholder conflicts.

Topic weight from 2027: 6–9% of Level I, about 11 to 16 of the 180 questions.

10 questions · Level I format · about 15 minutes

Choose an answer to see the explanation.

  1. A project costs 1,000 today and returns 400 at the end of each of the next three years. At a 10% cost of capital, what is its NPV, and should it be accepted?

    Answer: B. PV of inflows = 400 × [1 − 1.10^−3] / 0.10 = 994.74, so NPV = 994.74 − 1,000 = −5.26. A negative NPV destroys value, so reject. +200 ignores discounting altogether.

  2. Two mutually exclusive projects rank differently by NPV and by IRR. Which project should a value-maximising company choose?

    Answer: C. NPV measures the value added in currency and assumes cash flows are reinvested at the cost of capital. IRR can mislead when projects differ in size or timing, so NPV wins when the two conflict.

  3. A company is financed 60% by equity costing 12% and 40% by debt with a pre-tax cost of 6%. The tax rate is 25%. What is its WACC?

    Answer: A. WACC = 0.60 × 12% + 0.40 × 6% × (1 − 0.25) = 7.2% + 1.8% = 9.0%. 9.6% forgets that interest is tax-deductible.

  4. The risk-free rate is 4%, the equity risk premium is 5% and a stock's beta is 1.2. What is its cost of equity under the CAPM?

    Answer: C. Cost of equity = 4% + 1.2 × 5% = 10.0%. 9.0% ignores beta; 10.8% wrongly multiplies the whole market return (9%) by beta.

  5. Sales rise by 10% and operating income (EBIT) rises by 25%. What is the company's degree of operating leverage?

    Answer: A. DOL = % change in EBIT / % change in sales = 25% / 10% = 2.5. High fixed operating costs make EBIT swing more than sales.

  6. A company holds inventory for 50 days, collects receivables in 40 days and pays suppliers in 30 days. What is its cash conversion cycle?

    Answer: B. Cash conversion cycle = days of inventory + days of receivables − days of payables = 50 + 40 − 30 = 60 days: the time cash is tied up in operations.

  7. A project costs 500 and returns 200 a year for three years. What is its payback period?

    Answer: B. After two years 400 has come back; the remaining 100 takes half of the third year's 200. Payback = 2.5 years. Payback ignores the time value of money and everything after the cut-off, which is why it is only a rough screen.

  8. Under Modigliani and Miller's first proposition without taxes, increasing a company's debt:

    Answer: A. With no taxes or costs of distress, value depends only on the operating cash flows. Cheaper debt is exactly offset by riskier, more expensive equity, so the WACC and firm value do not change.

  9. A staggered (classified) board, where only some directors are elected each year, most likely:

    Answer: A. Because only a fraction of seats come up each year, a bidder cannot replace the board in one vote. That can protect managers from the discipline of takeovers, which is why many governance codes view staggered boards critically.

  10. A supplier offers terms of 2/10, net 30: a 2% discount if you pay within 10 days, otherwise full payment in 30. What is the effective annual cost of NOT taking the discount?

    Answer: C. Skipping the discount means borrowing 98 for 20 extra days at a cost of 2. Per period: 2 / 98 = 2.04%; there are 365 / 20 = 18.25 such periods a year, so (1.0204)^18.25 − 1 = 44.6%. Trade credit is very expensive.

Three things to remember for corporate issuers

  • NPV beats IRR whenever the two disagree on mutually exclusive projects.
  • Use the after-tax cost of debt in the WACC.
  • The cash conversion cycle adds inventory and receivable days and subtracts payable days.

How to use these questions

Answer each question before you look at the explanation, and time yourself: the real exam allows about 90 seconds per question. Mark every question you got right for the wrong reason as wrong. When you miss one, write down the idea behind it rather than the answer, and come back to the page in a week.

These questions are written by First Principles to match the style and level of the exam. They are not taken from CFA Institute's curriculum or mock exams, which you should also use: they are included with your exam registration.

No. CFA Institute does not publish past exam questions. These are original questions written to match the Level I format: a short stem and three answer options, testing one idea each.
