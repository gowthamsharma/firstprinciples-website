CFA vs CPA, CFP, ACCA and CA: how the finance qualifications compare

The CFA is for investment decisions; CPA, ACCA and CA are for accounting and audit; the CFP is for personal financial planning. Here is how they differ in exams, experience and careers.

3 min read  /  By Gowtham Kumar

Finance has several respected qualifications, and they are often compared as if they were rivals. Mostly they are not: each one prepares you for a different kind of work.

The quick comparison

CFA CPA (US) CFP ACCA CA (India)
Awarded by CFA Institute US state boards (exam by AICPA) CFP Board (US) ACCA ICAI
Core focus investments accounting, audit, tax personal financial planning accounting, audit, finance accounting, audit, tax
Exams 3 levels 4 sections 1 exam (170 questions) up to 13 exams 3 stages: Foundation, Intermediate, Final
Experience 4,000 hours over 36+ months about 1–2 years, by state 6,000 hours (or 4,000 as an apprentice) 36 months practical training (articleship)
Typical careers research, portfolio and wealth management audit, tax, controllership, CFO track financial adviser, wealth planner audit, accounting, finance roles worldwide audit, tax, finance in India and abroad

CFA vs CPA

The CPA is the US licence for public accountants. Its exam has three core sections (auditing, financial accounting and reporting, and regulation/tax) plus one discipline section chosen by the candidate. Licensing also needs education (traditionally 150 credit hours, with newer routes allowing 120 plus extra experience) and supervised experience. Choose the CPA for audit, tax or corporate accounting; choose the CFA for investment analysis. People in equity research with an accounting background sometimes hold both.

CFA vs CFP

The CFP is about advising individuals and families: budgeting, insurance, retirement, tax and estate planning. It needs a bachelor's degree, approved coursework, one six-hour exam and 6,000 hours of experience. The CFA goes much deeper into markets and portfolios; the CFP goes broader into a client's whole financial life. Wealth managers sometimes earn both.

CFA vs ACCA

The ACCA is a global accounting qualification: up to 13 exams (with exemptions for relevant degrees), an ethics module and 36 months of experience. It is widely recognised in the UK, Europe, the Middle East, Africa and Asia. Choose it for accounting, audit or finance-function careers; choose the CFA for investment roles.

CFA vs a chartered accountancy (CA)

Most countries have their own chartered accountancy route: CA in India, the ACA of ICAEW in England and Wales, CA ANZ in Australia and New Zealand, CPA Canada, and the Wirtschaftsprüfer in Germany, among others. They share a pattern of exams plus supervised practical training. India's CA, for example, has Foundation, Intermediate and Final exams with articleship. CAs often add the CFA to move into investment banking, research or fund management, and the accounting depth of a CA is a real advantage in the CFA's financial statement analysis.

How to choose

Ask one question: what decisions will you make in your job? Investment decisions point to the CFA, assurance and reporting to CPA, ACCA or CA, advice to individuals to the CFP.

Questions people ask

Can I do the CFA after CA or ACCA?

Yes, and it is a common combination. Your accounting knowledge will make Level I financial reporting much easier.

Is the CFA harder than the CPA?

They are hard in different ways. The CFA covers more investment content over three long exams; the CPA is more accounting-focused, with more sections taken closer together.

CFA or CFP for wealth management?

For advising individuals in the US, the CFP is the standard. For managing portfolios or investment products, the CFA carries more weight. Many senior wealth managers hold both.
