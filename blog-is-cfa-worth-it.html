Is the CFA worth it? An honest answer for 2026

For investment careers the charter is still one of the strongest signals you can earn, at a fraction of an MBA's cost. For careers outside investing, the same hours may be better spent elsewhere.

3 min read  /  By Gowtham Kumar

Search "CFA" and Google suggests both "why CFA is important" and "is CFA useless". Both camps have a point. Whether the charter is worth it depends on what you want to do with it.

What it costs you

  • Time: around 900 hours over two to four years, usually alongside a full-time job.
  • Money: USD 3,520 to 4,570 in exam fees, plus materials and perhaps a retake.
  • Opportunity: evenings and weekends you could spend on other skills, a side project or rest.

What you get

  • Breadth and depth in investments: accounting, valuation, fixed income, derivatives, portfolio management and ethics, taught to a consistent standard.
  • A signal employers recognise worldwide. In research, asset management and wealth management, many job adverts list the charter as preferred or required.
  • Discipline proof. Passing three hard exams while working tells an employer something about you beyond the content.
  • A professional network through CFA societies in many cities.

When it is clearly worth it

  • You work, or want to work, in equity or credit research, portfolio management, wealth management, risk or investment consulting.
  • You are in finance but outside the investment side and want to move in.
  • You come from another field (engineering, science, law) and need a recognised way to show finance knowledge.

When it may not be

  • Your target role values a different credential: audit or tax (CPA, ACCA, CA), US personal financial planning (CFP), or corporate leadership and career switches (often an MBA).
  • You need results in months, not years.
  • The work you want depends mostly on deals and networks, as in much of investment banking.

"Is the CFA still relevant?"

Tools change, with more data, automation and AI in research, but the core questions the curriculum teaches have not: what something is worth, what could go wrong, and whose interests come first. The program has also added practical modules, including programming and data skills, alongside the exams. Combining the charter with data skills makes a stronger profile than either alone.

Alternatives to consider

  • FRM for risk management careers.
  • CPA, ACCA or CA for accounting and audit.
  • CFP for financial planning in the US.
  • An MBA for a broad career switch or leadership track.
  • Smaller certificates (such as the CFA Institute Investment Foundations certificate or ESG certificates) for a lighter introduction.

Questions people ask

Is the CFA still worth it with AI?

Yes for investment careers. AI speeds up gathering and summarising information, but judging value, risk and suitability still needs the understanding the curriculum builds, and employers still use the charter as a filter.

Is the CFA useless without experience?

Not useless, but incomplete. You need 4,000 hours of relevant work to hold the charter, and employers value the exams most alongside real experience.

What is the CFA equivalent to?

It is a professional designation, not a degree. Some universities and regulators recognise it for credit or exemptions, but it does not formally equal a master's degree everywhere.
