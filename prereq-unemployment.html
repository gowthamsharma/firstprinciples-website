Unemployment and the labour market

By Gowtham KumarLesson 42 of 50

Not all unemployment is a sign of weakness. Separating frictional, structural and cyclical unemployment shows how much slack the economy really has.

Unemployment rate = unemployed / labour force. Only cyclical unemployment measures the output gap.

From first principles

The labour force is everyone working or actively looking for work. Someone who wants a job but has stopped looking (a discouraged worker) is not counted, so the unemployment rate can fall in a weak economy simply because people give up. The participation rate (labour force / working-age population) catches this.

Three kinds of unemployment have different causes:

  • Frictional: people between jobs; searching takes time even in a strong economy.
  • Structural: skills or locations do not match the available jobs, after technology or trade changes. It is slow to fix.
  • Cyclical: not enough demand, so it rises in recessions and falls in expansions.

Frictional plus structural unemployment make up the natural rate: unemployment when the economy is at potential output. Pushing unemployment below it tends to raise wage growth and inflation.

Worked example

Population of working age 1,000; employed 600; unemployed and searching 40; discouraged 20. Labour force = 640; unemployment rate = 40 / 640 = 6.25%; participation = 64%. If the 20 discouraged workers had kept looking, the rate would be 60 / 660 = 9.1%.

Exam traps

  • Unemployment is a lagging indicator: firms hold on to staff into a downturn and hire cautiously in recoveries.
  • Productivity (output per hour) often rises early in recoveries, because output recovers before hiring does.

Questions people ask

What are the types of unemployment?

Frictional (between jobs), structural (skills or locations that no longer match jobs) and cyclical (caused by a weak economy). Only the cyclical part responds directly to stimulus.

Why is unemployment called a lagging indicator?

Firms wait to be sure a downturn is real before laying people off, and wait to be sure of a recovery before hiring. So unemployment turns after the economy does.

What is the natural rate of unemployment?

The rate when there is no cyclical unemployment, only frictional and structural. Pushing unemployment below it for long tends to raise inflation.
