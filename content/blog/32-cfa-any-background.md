The CFA Program does not require a finance or business degree. You can register if you have a **bachelor's degree**, are in the **final year** of one, or have **4,000 hours of work experience and/or higher education** over at least three years. You also need a valid passport, and the exams are in English. Everything else is preparation.

## Background by background

### Commerce, business and economics (BCom, BBA, BA Economics)

**Head start:** accounting, economics and corporate finance basics.
**Gaps:** statistics and calculator fluency; derivatives.
**Extra time:** little; often less than 300 hours at Level I.
**Good fits:** research, corporate finance, wealth management, credit.

### Engineering and IT

**Head start:** maths, statistics, programming.
**Gaps:** accounting, economics, finance vocabulary.
**Extra time:** 4–6 weeks of foundations before the main plan.
**Good fits:** quant, risk, data, fintech, sector research. See our [guide for engineers](blog-cfa-for-engineers.html).

### Science (physics, chemistry, biology, mathematics)

**Head start:** quantitative reasoning; for mathematicians, statistics and probability.
**Gaps:** accounting, economics, markets.
**Extra time:** 4–6 weeks.
**Good fits:** quant research, risk, and research on healthcare, chemicals, energy or materials companies.

### Medicine, pharmacy and healthcare

**Head start:** analytical discipline, study stamina, and sector knowledge that investors value.
**Gaps:** almost all finance topics; maths may need a refresher.
**Extra time:** 6–8 weeks of foundations; plan 350+ hours for Level I.
**Good fits:** healthcare and biotech equity research, healthcare investing in private equity or venture capital, life-science fund analysis.

### Law

**Head start:** reading dense text precisely, which helps with ethics and with tricky question wording.
**Gaps:** quantitative methods, accounting, fixed income.
**Extra time:** 6–8 weeks, with extra calculator practice.
**Good fits:** compliance, regulation, fund structuring, private markets and restructuring roles where legal and financial skills overlap.

### Arts, humanities and social sciences

**Head start:** writing and communication, an advantage in Level III's written answers and in client roles.
**Gaps:** maths, accounting, economics.
**Extra time:** 6–10 weeks of foundations; be generous with the plan.
**Good fits:** wealth management, investor relations, research with strong written output, ESG and stewardship roles.

### No degree, with work experience

You qualify with 4,000 hours of work experience and/or higher education over at least three sequential years. The work does not have to be in finance for registration, although the **charter** later needs 4,000 hours of relevant investment experience.

## The common plan for any non-finance candidate

1. **Foundations first (4–8 weeks):** the three financial statements, accruals, basic economics and compounding. Our free [prerequisite concepts](prerequisites.html) are written for exactly this.
2. **Calculator early:** learn time value and statistics on the [BA II Plus](blog-ba-ii-plus-calculator-guide.html) before you meet them in the curriculum.
3. **Then the normal Level I plan:** 300+ hours over five to six months, with mocks at the end. Check your readiness with our [self-check](blog-ready-for-cfa-level-1.html).
4. **Build relevant experience** alongside the exams, so the charter is not delayed by the experience requirement.

## Does a non-finance background hurt in jobs?

Less than people think, if you can explain why you are moving and show the skills. A doctor covering biotech stocks, an engineer covering semiconductors or a lawyer in fund structuring each brings something finance graduates do not. The charter gives employers proof that you have learned the investment toolkit too.

## Questions people ask

### Can I do the CFA without a commerce background?

Yes. Any bachelor's degree qualifies. Expect to spend extra weeks on accounting and economics at the start.

### Can a doctor or medical student do the CFA?

Yes. Healthcare knowledge is valuable in healthcare and biotech research and investing. Plan extra time for the finance foundations.

### Can I do the CFA after BA or BSc?

Yes, after any bachelor's degree, or in its final year. Arts graduates often find Level III's written format easier; science graduates find the quantitative parts easier.

### Can I do the CFA without a degree?

Yes, if you have 4,000 hours of work experience and/or higher education over at least three sequential years.
