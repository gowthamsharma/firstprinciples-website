"How long does the CFA take?" has two answers: how long the **exams** take, and how long until you can call yourself a **charterholder**. The second is always longer.


[[figure]]

## How long each level takes to study for

Candidates who pass typically report **300 hours or more per level**. At 12 to 15 hours a week, that is about **five to six months** of study before each exam. Some people with a strong finance background need less for Level I; Level II often needs more.

## How fast you can move through the exams

The calendar limits how quickly you can go:

- Level I is offered in **four** windows a year, Level II in **three**, and Level III in **two** (February and August).
- You need your result before you can sensibly sit the next level, and results arrive **five to nine weeks** after a window closes.
- If you fail, you must wait **at least six months** before retaking the same level.

The quickest possible sequence is Level I in February, Level II in August and Level III the next February, **about a year**, but only if each result arrives before the next registration deadline closes. A more comfortable fast track puts Level III the following August, **about 18 months**. Many candidates take **two to four years**, often because of one retake.

## Can you do the CFA in one year?

Only just, and rarely. It needs the February–August–February sequence, a pass at every level, results arriving before each registration deadline, and around 900 hours of study in twelve months. Plan for 18 months or more, especially if you work full time.

## Then the work experience

To use the charter you also need **4,000 hours of relevant work over at least 36 months**. The good news: it can be built **before or during** the exams. Many candidates already have it by the time they pass Level III.

## How to keep the timeline short

- **Register early** for each exam so you are not pushed to a later window.
- **Start the next level's reading** while you wait for a result; if you passed, you are ahead, and if you failed you have reviewed the basics.
- **Pass first time.** One retake adds six months or more.

## Hours per week decide the months

| Hours per week | Months for 300 hours | Months for 350 hours |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | about 9 | about 10 |
| 10 | about 7 | about 8 |
| 12 | about 6 | about 7 |
| 15 | about 5 | about 6 |
| 20 | about 4 | about 4.5 |

## What usually happens, not just what is possible

We simulated 100,000 candidates through the exam windows and retake rules. With pass chances like the recent averages, **about half finish all three exams within three years** of their first Level I attempt, and fewer than one in ten pass all three first time. Well-prepared candidates typically finish in **18 months to two years**. See [how long it really takes to pass](blog-cfa-time-to-pass.html) for the full model.

## Questions people ask

### How many months should I study for CFA Level 1?

About five to six months at 12 to 15 hours a week, which adds up to the 300-plus hours most passing candidates report.

### What is the fastest you can finish all three levels?

About a year at the very least (February, August, February), and more realistically around 18 months, assuming every level is passed first time.

### How long is the CFA valid?

Once earned, the charter is held through CFA Institute membership; you keep using it as long as you remain a member in good standing and pay annual dues.
