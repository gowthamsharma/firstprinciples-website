The CFA Program is long: three levels over two to four years, with the same ideas returning at every level in harder forms. That changes what "studying efficiently" means.


[[figure]]

## The problem with memorising

A memorised fact is fast to learn and fast to forget. It also breaks when the exam asks it from an unusual angle: a vignette that inverts the usual setup, or a constructed-response question that asks you to *justify*. Level II and Level III are built from exactly those questions.

## What first principles means in practice

1. **Find the bedrock.** For bond pricing: money later is worth less than money now. For accounting: every transaction affects at least two accounts, so the balance sheet always balances.
2. **Rebuild the idea step by step**, drawing each step. If a step does not follow from the last, you have found the gap in your understanding.
3. **Write the formula last.** By then it is a summary of what you already see, so you can rebuild it in the exam if memory fails.
4. **Predict before you check.** Before working a practice question, guess the direction of the answer: does the price rise or fall? Is this a DTA or a DTL? Being wrong in a prediction teaches more than being right in a calculation.

## An example

Why does LIFO lower profit when prices rise? Not "because LIFO is conservative". When prices rise, the newest units cost the most. LIFO assumes the newest units are sold first, so the most expensive costs go to cost of sales, and profit falls. Once you see that, the LIFO reserve, LIFO liquidations and the tax effect all follow without memorising anything new. ([See it worked through](prereq-lifo-reserve.html).)

## The cost, and why it is worth it

First-principles study is slower per topic: maybe 20% more time on the first pass. It pays back at revision, because there is less to re-memorise, and again at the next level, because the foundations are still there.

That is the method behind every lesson on this site, starting with the [prerequisite concepts](prerequisites.html).

## A second example: why deferred tax exists

Memorised version: "a deferred tax liability arises when taxable income is lower than accounting profit." Few people remember which way round that goes.

From first principles: companies keep **two sets of rules**, one for investors (accounting) and one for the tax authority. If the tax rules let a company depreciate a machine faster, it pays **less tax now** than its accounting profit suggests. That tax is not avoided, only postponed: it will be paid later when the tax depreciation runs out. A tax you will pay later is a **liability**. So faster tax depreciation creates a deferred tax liability, and you can now derive the rule instead of memorising it. Our [deferred tax lesson](prereq-deferred-tax.html) walks through it with a diagram.

## How to practise it

- **Ask "why must this be true?"** before writing down any formula.
- **Draw it:** a timeline, a balance sheet, a supply and demand graph.
- **Predict, then check:** before calculating, decide whether the answer should be bigger or smaller, and by roughly how much.
- **Explain it to someone else** in two sentences. If you cannot, you do not own it yet.
- **Then drill questions**, so understanding becomes speed.

## Questions people ask

### What does learning from first principles mean?

Starting from a few facts that cannot be otherwise and reasoning step by step to the result, instead of memorising the result. You can rebuild the idea when you forget the formula.

### Does first principles take longer than memorising?

At first, yes. But the same ideas return at all three levels, so understanding once saves relearning twice. It also helps with questions that twist the usual setup.

### How can I practise first-principles thinking?

Before reading a solution, predict the answer and say why. After a wrong answer, ask which basic fact you missed rather than which formula.

### Is first principles enough to pass?

Understanding is necessary but you still need speed. Pair it with timed practice so that the reasoning becomes fast enough for exam conditions.
