Level I is a test of **breadth under time pressure**: 180 standalone questions, about 90 seconds each, across ten topics. Most candidates who fail know enough. What lets them down is uneven coverage or slow, careless answering.


[[figure]]

## Cover everything, weighted sensibly

Every topic is examined, and the weights are ranges, so you cannot predict where the hard questions will fall. Learn every reading once, then give extra practice time to the four largest areas: financial statement analysis, fixed income, equities and (from 2027) quantitative methods. Ethics is 10–15% in 2027; it is very learnable with enough questions.

## Master the calculator early

Only the **Texas Instruments BA II Plus** (including the Professional) and the **HP 12C** family are allowed. Pick one in week one and use it for every practice question. Learn these until they are automatic:

- time value of money (N, I/Y, PV, PMT, FV) and the sign convention;
- the cash flow worksheet for NPV and IRR;
- statistics (mean and standard deviation) and memory registers;
- setting the decimal places (four or more) and, for the BA II Plus, payments per year to 1.

## Answer like the exam asks

- **Read the last line first.** Know what is being asked before you read the setup.
- **Three options, so eliminate.** Discarding one wrong option raises a blind guess from 33% to 50%.
- **Never leave a blank.** There is no penalty for wrong answers.
- **Bank time on easy questions.** If you are not getting anywhere after two minutes, guess, flag it and move on.
- **Watch for "except", "least likely" and "most appropriate".** Many wrong answers are correct statements that answer a different question.

## Practise in the exam's rhythm

From the practice block onwards, do questions in timed sets of 30 or more (45 minutes per 30 questions) rather than one at a time. For the final month, sit full mocks of 90 + 90 questions under exam conditions.

## A 24-week Level I plan

| Weeks | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Ethics (first pass), quantitative methods and the calculator |
| 3–4 | Quantitative methods, economics |
| 5–9 | Financial statement analysis |
| 10 | Corporate issuers |
| 11–12 | Equity investments |
| 13–14 | Fixed income |
| 15 | Derivatives, alternative investments |
| 16 | Portfolio management |
| 17–18 | Mixed question practice, weakest topics first |
| 19–24 | Full mock exams with review; ethics second pass |

## Questions people ask

### Is CFA Level 1 hard?

It is broad rather than deep. Most questions are short and fair, but ten topics in about 270 minutes leave little room for gaps. Fewer than half of candidates pass in a typical session, mostly because of uneven preparation.

### Which Level I topics should I focus on?

All of them, but give extra practice to the heaviest: ethics, financial statements, equities, bonds and, from 2027, quantitative methods. Ethics is especially worth mastering because close results often turn on it.

### How fast do I need to answer Level I questions?

About 90 seconds each. In practice, many conceptual questions take 45 seconds, which buys time for calculations. Flag anything that runs past two minutes and come back to it.

### Which calculator should I use?

Only the approved models are allowed: the BA II Plus family or the HP 12C family. Pick one early and use it for every practice question, so the keystrokes become automatic.
