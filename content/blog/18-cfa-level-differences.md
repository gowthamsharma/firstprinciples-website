The three CFA exams share a name and a curriculum style, but they test very different things. Knowing how they differ helps you change your study method at each step, which is where many candidates who passed one level stumble on the next.


[[figure]]

## Level I: the tools

- **Format:** 180 standalone multiple-choice questions in two sessions of about 2¼ hours.
- **Content:** ten broad topic areas, from ethics and quantitative methods to derivatives and portfolio management.
- **Skill:** recall and apply. Most questions test one idea at a time.
- **Typical trap:** breadth. A gap in any heavy topic is costly.
- **How to study:** cover everything once, then practise many short questions.

## Level II: valuation

- **Format:** 22 case studies ("item sets"), each with four multiple-choice questions, about 4 hours 24 minutes in total.
- **Content:** fewer topics but much deeper, with heavy emphasis on financial statements, equity valuation and fixed income.
- **Skill:** apply and analyse. One case mixes several ideas and gives more data than you need.
- **Typical trap:** reading speed and depth. Long vignettes punish slow reading and shallow understanding.
- **How to study:** practise whole item sets under time, about 12 minutes each.

## Level III: portfolio management

- **Format:** 11 item sets plus 11 constructed-response (written-answer) sets, in two sessions.
- **Content:** asset allocation, portfolio construction, performance measurement, derivatives and risk, ethics, plus your chosen **pathway** (about a third of the exam).
- **Skill:** judge and recommend. You explain and justify decisions for a client.
- **Typical trap:** writing too much or answering a different question. Command words matter.
- **How to study:** write timed answers and compare them with official guideline answers.

## What carries over

Ideas build on each other. Financial statement analysis and economics run through all three levels; time value of money and statistics never go away; ethics appears at every level. Understanding these foundations properly at Level I saves time at Levels II and III (see the [prerequisite concepts](prerequisites.html)).

## The three exams at a glance

| | Level I | Level II | Level III |
|---|---|---|---|
| Question format | 180 multiple choice | 22 item sets (vignettes), 88 questions | 11 item sets + 11 written (constructed-response) sets |
| Time | 2 × 2 h 15 min | 4 h 24 min | 4 h 24 min |
| What it tests | Knowing the tools | Applying them to value assets | Building and defending a portfolio for a client |
| Exam windows | Feb, May, Aug, Nov | May, Aug, Nov | Feb, Aug |
| Recent pass rate | about 43% | about 45% | about 47% |
| Hardest part for most | Breadth and speed | Depth and volume | Writing under time pressure |

## How your study should change

- **Level I:** cover everything once, then drill questions. Speed matters.
- **Level II:** fewer topics, much deeper. Learn which model fits which situation. Practise full item sets, not single questions.
- **Level III:** practise **writing**: short, exact answers to command words like *justify* and *recommend*, marked against official guideline answers.

## How long each takes

CFA Institute recommends about **300 hours of study per level**, and most people who pass report at least that much. Plan more time for Level II than you did for Level I: it is the level candidates most often describe as the biggest jump.


## What changes when you move up a level

### From Level I to Level II

- **From breadth to depth.** Level II has the same topic names, but each goes two or three steps further. Free cash flow, residual income, pensions and bond trees are new, and they rely on Level I tools being automatic.
- **From one question to one case.** Each question sits inside a vignette, so you read about 22 short cases in one sitting.
- **Accounting becomes analysis.** At Level I you learn how a transaction is recorded. At Level II you adjust the statements of a real company and decide what its earnings really are.
- **What helps most:** a refresher of Level I financial statement analysis and fixed income before you start Level II.

### From Level II to Level III

- **From valuing assets to managing portfolios.** Level III rarely asks what a single security is worth. It asks what a client should hold, and why.
- **From choosing to writing.** Half the exam is constructed response. You must state a decision and justify it in a few lines, under time pressure.
- **From numbers to judgement.** Calculations remain, but most points come from applying a framework (the investment policy statement, an asset allocation approach, a hedging decision) to the facts of a case.
- **What helps most:** writing practice from early in your preparation, marked against official guideline answers.

## How the study load compares

| | Level I | Level II | Level III |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical study time | 300 hours | 300–350 hours | 300 hours, a quarter of it writing practice |
| Number of topic areas | 10 | 10 | 5 core areas + 1 pathway |
| Share of calculation questions | High | High | Moderate |
| Formula load | About 200 | About 250, including Level I | About 120–150 |
| Main skill | Recall and apply | Analyse a case | Recommend and justify |

## Gaps between levels

You can register for the next level once you have passed the previous one, and the exam windows allow you to move quickly: for example, Level I in February, Level II in August and Level III the following February or August. Most candidates leave about six months between exams to fit in 300 hours of study around work. Our [timeline guide](blog-how-long-cfa.html) shows the fastest and the typical routes.

## Questions people ask

### Is Level II harder than Level I?

Most candidates say yes, because the material is deeper and every question sits inside a case. Pass rates are not very different, partly because the candidate group at Level II has already passed Level I.

### Is Level III easier than Level II?

It is different rather than easier: less calculation, more judgement and writing. Candidates who are strong in calculation can find the written answers the toughest part.

### Can I skip a level?

No. Each level must be passed before you can register for the next.


### Which CFA level has the most formulas?

Level II, because it adds equity, fixed income and derivatives valuation models on top of the Level I toolkit. Level III has fewer formulas but asks you to justify how you use them.

### Is Level III easier than Level II?

Not easier, but different. Pass rates are similar. Candidates who write clearly and practise constructed responses often find Level III more manageable; those who struggle to write under time pressure find it the hardest.
