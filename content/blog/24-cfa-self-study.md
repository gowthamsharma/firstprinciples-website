Classroom coaching is popular in many countries, from New York and London to Mumbai, Karachi and Lagos, but it is not required. The CFA Program was designed for working professionals who study **in their own time**, and many candidates pass every level without a class. What matters is how you study.


[[figure]]

## What you need to self-study well

1. **A structured plan.** Around 300 hours per level, spread over five to six months, with learning, practice and mock phases (see the [study plan](blog-study-plan.html)).
2. **The curriculum or a good summary.** CFA Institute's curriculum comes with your exam fee. Many self-studiers add condensed notes to save time.
3. **Lots of practice questions.** Questions turn reading into marks. Use the official question bank, and add a third-party bank if you run out.
4. **Full mock exams under timed conditions**, at least three to five in the final six weeks, each followed by careful review.
5. **A way to get unstuck**: video explanations, a study group or online forums for the topics you cannot crack alone.

## Self-study without a finance background

Engineers, scientists and other non-finance candidates pass every year. Expect to spend **extra hours at Level I** on financial statement analysis, economics and fixed income. Build those foundations first; understanding them properly pays off at every later level. Our [prerequisite concepts](prerequisites.html) explain them from the basics.

## How to pass first time

- **Start early** and keep a buffer of two or three weeks.
- **Rotate topics** so nothing goes stale.
- **Do questions every week**, not only at the end.
- **Keep an error log** and look for patterns: knowledge gaps, misreading or timing.
- **Take ethics seriously** throughout.
- **Rehearse exam day** with timed mocks, the approved calculator and realistic breaks.

## When coaching helps

A class can help if you need **external deadlines** to stay on track, if you learn better by **asking questions live**, or if you have **failed before** and need a structured reset. Choose it for the gap it fills, not by default.

## A weekly self-study rhythm

| Day | What | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | New reading | 1.5 h |
| Tuesday | Same reading, end-of-chapter questions | 1.5 h |
| Wednesday | Rest or catch-up | — |
| Thursday | New reading | 1.5 h |
| Friday | Questions on this week's topics | 1 h |
| Saturday | Longer block: new reading and a mixed question set | 4 h |
| Sunday | Review mistakes from the week, flashcards, plan next week | 3 h |

That is about **12–13 hours**, enough for 300 hours in six months. Adjust the days, not the total.

## Self-study vs coaching, honestly

| | Self-study | Coaching or a structured course |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Lowest | Higher |
| Flexibility | Total | Fixed timetable |
| Discipline | All on you | External deadlines |
| Getting unstuck | Forums, videos, study groups | A teacher to ask |
| Best for | Organised people with steady time | People who need structure, or who failed before |

## Questions people ask

### Can I clear the CFA without coaching?

Yes. Many candidates pass all three levels by self-study with the official curriculum, a question bank and mock exams.

### Can I do the CFA online?

The study can be entirely online, and CFA Institute's learning platform is online. The exams themselves are taken in person at test centres.

### How do I pass CFA Level 1 on the first attempt?

Plan 300-plus hours, cover every topic once, practise many questions, take full mocks in the last six weeks and review every mistake. Give ethics and the heaviest topics extra time.
