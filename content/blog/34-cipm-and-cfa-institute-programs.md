CFA Institute runs several programs besides the CFA Program. Some are serious credentials in their own right; others are short certificates that show knowledge in one area. Choosing well can make you more employable faster, especially alongside CFA Level I or while you build experience.

[[figure]]

## The CIPM: a credential for performance and manager evaluation

The **Certificate in Investment Performance Measurement (CIPM)** is CFA Institute's second designation. It certifies the skills used to answer a question every investor asks: *how well did this portfolio or manager really do, and why?*

**What it covers:**

- **Return measurement:** time-weighted and money-weighted returns and when each is appropriate.
- **Performance attribution:** splitting returns into allocation, selection and other effects, so you know whether skill or luck produced them.
- **Risk-adjusted performance:** Sharpe and information ratios, tracking error, drawdowns.
- **Benchmarks and manager selection:** judging whether a benchmark is fair and choosing among managers.
- **The GIPS standards:** the Global Investment Performance Standards that firms follow when presenting results to clients. CFA Institute reports that more than 1,600 organisations use them.
- **Ethics** for performance professionals.

**How it works:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Levels | Two exams, Level I and Level II |
| Exam windows | March and September each year |
| Fees | From USD 675 per exam (early registration) |
| Study time | About 155 hours per level |
| Typical time to finish | About 18 months |
| Entry requirements | None for Level I |
| CFA shortcut | Charterholders and candidates who have passed CFA Level III can go straight to CIPM Level II |
| Pass rates (March 2026) | 31% at Level I, 44% at Level II |
| To use the designation | Relevant professional experience, CFA Institute membership and 15 hours of continuing education a year |

**Who it is for:** performance analysts, investment consultants, manager-research and fund-selection teams, risk analysts, client reporting teams, compliance officers, and portfolio managers who present results to clients.

**Why it helps your career:**

- It is **specialist and rare**: far fewer people hold the CIPM than the CFA, so it differentiates you in performance and consulting teams.
- It is **directly usable at work**: attribution and GIPS compliance are daily tasks in these teams.
- It is **shorter** than the CFA Program, so it fits early in a career.
- For charterholders, it is a quick add-on (one exam) for moving into consulting or manager selection.

**CIPM vs CFA:** the CFA Program is broad and covers how to *make* investment decisions; the CIPM is narrow and covers how to *evaluate* them. They complement each other; they are not substitutes.

## The shorter certificates

These are online, self-paced courses with an assessment, not designations. List prices as of November 2026:

| Certificate | Level | Study time | Fee (USD) | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Investment Foundations | Foundational | 35–65 hours | 350 | Non-investment staff at financial firms, students, anyone testing interest before CFA Level I |
| Sustainable Investing | Foundational | about 100 hours, one exam | 890 | ESG analysts, stewardship, product and sales roles at asset managers |
| Climate Investing | Intermediate | about 100 hours, five courses | 1,590 | Analysts and portfolio managers integrating climate risk |
| Private Markets and Alternative Investments | Foundational | 70–90 hours | 890 | Anyone moving towards private equity, private credit, real estate or infrastructure |
| Private Equity | Intermediate | about 125 hours | 890 | Fund analysts, LP investment teams, deal support |
| Advanced Private Equity | Advanced | about 100 hours | 890 | Experienced private equity professionals |

Prices change and discounts are common; check before you register.

## Which combination fits which job?

| Your goal | A strong combination |
|---|---|
| First job in investments, no finance background | Investment Foundations, then CFA Level I |
| Performance, consulting, manager research | CFA Level I–II, then CIPM (or CFA charter, then CIPM Level II) |
| ESG or stewardship roles | CFA Level I + Sustainable Investing certificate |
| Climate-focused investing | CFA Program + Climate Investing certificate |
| Private equity or private credit | CFA Program (Private Markets pathway at Level III) + Private Markets or Private Equity certificate |
| Risk management | CFA Program and/or FRM (from GARP), plus CIPM for performance risk |

A certificate will not replace the CFA charter for portfolio management or research roles. It is best used to **show focus** in a specific area, or to **bridge** the time before and between CFA levels.

## Questions people ask

### What is the CIPM?

The Certificate in Investment Performance Measurement, a two-level CFA Institute designation for professionals who measure, attribute and present investment performance, including under the GIPS standards.

### Is the CIPM worth it?

For performance analysts, investment consultants and manager-selection teams, yes: it is specialist, practical and less common than the CFA. For research or portfolio management roles, the CFA charter matters more.

### Can I do the CIPM and the CFA together?

Yes. Many people do the CIPM after passing CFA Level III, which lets them skip CIPM Level I and take only Level II.

### Is the Investment Foundations certificate useful?

It is a low-cost way to learn the investment industry's basics and show interest, especially for people in operations, technology, sales or support roles. It is not a substitute for CFA Level I.
