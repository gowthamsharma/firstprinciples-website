> \[MC = \frac{\Delta TC}{\Delta Q}\]
>
> Marginal cost cuts average variable and average total cost at their minimum points.

## From first principles

In the short run some costs are **fixed** (rent, the factory, insurance) and do not change with output; others are **variable** (materials, hourly labour, energy).

\[ TC = FC + VC \]

Spread over output:

\[ AFC = \frac{FC}{Q} \qquad AVC = \frac{VC}{Q} \qquad ATC = \frac{TC}{Q} = AFC + AVC \qquad MC = \frac{\Delta TC}{\Delta Q} = \frac{\Delta VC}{\Delta Q} \]

- **AFC** falls forever, as fixed costs spread over more units.
- **MC** eventually rises, because of [diminishing marginal returns](prereq-marginal-returns.html): each extra unit needs more and more of the variable input.
- **AVC and ATC are U-shaped**: they fall while MC is below them and rise once MC is above them.

[[figure]]

Why does MC cut AVC and ATC at their lowest points? Think of grades: if your next exam scores **below** your average, the average falls; **above**, it rises. Average cost bottoms out exactly where the marginal unit costs the same as the average.

## Worked example: a cost table

Fixed cost is 100.

| Output | Variable cost | Total cost | MC | AFC | AVC | ATC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 40 | 140 | 40 | 100.0 | 40.0 | 140.0 |
| 2 | 70 | 170 | 30 | 50.0 | 35.0 | 85.0 |
| 3 | 95 | 195 | 25 | 33.3 | 31.7 | 65.0 |
| 4 | 125 | 225 | 30 | 25.0 | 31.25 | 56.25 |
| 5 | 165 | 265 | 40 | 20.0 | 33.0 | 53.0 |
| 6 | 215 | 315 | 50 | 16.7 | 35.8 | 52.5 |
| 7 | 275 | 375 | 60 | 14.3 | 39.3 | 53.6 |

Read it with the marginal-average rule:

- **AVC is lowest at 4 units** (31.25). The 5th unit costs 40, above that average, so AVC rises.
- **ATC is lowest at 6 units** (52.5). The 6th unit costs 50, below the average of 53, so ATC is still falling; the 7th costs 60, above it, so ATC rises.
- ATC reaches its minimum at a **higher output** than AVC, because falling AFC keeps pulling ATC down for longer.

These numbers carry on into [profit maximisation and shutdown](prereq-profit-maximisation-shutdown.html).

## Exam traps

- **Fixed costs do not affect MC**, so they do not affect the short-run output decision; they only affect whether the firm makes a profit.
- **The gap between ATC and AVC is AFC**, which shrinks as output grows.
- **MC is calculated from changes in total (or variable) cost**, one unit at a time.
- **Sunk costs**, already spent and unrecoverable, are irrelevant to any future decision.

## Questions people ask

### What is the difference between fixed and variable costs?

Fixed costs stay the same whatever the output in the short run, such as rent. Variable costs rise and fall with output, such as materials. In the long run every cost is variable.

### Why does marginal cost eventually rise?

Because of diminishing marginal returns: with some inputs fixed, each extra unit of output needs more of the variable input, so it costs more to produce.

### Why does marginal cost cross average cost at its minimum?

Because an average falls whenever the next unit costs less than the average and rises whenever it costs more. The turning point is exactly where marginal cost equals average cost.
