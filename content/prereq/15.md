> The goods are identical; the costs are not. The method just decides which cost leaves the shelf first.

## From first principles

You bought 100 units at 10, then 100 at 12, then 100 at 14, and sold 200. The physical units are interchangeable, so accounting needs a rule for which costs count as sold:

- **FIFO (first in, first out)**: the oldest costs go to cost of sales. Ending inventory holds the newest costs, close to current prices.
- **LIFO (last in, first out)**: the newest costs go to cost of sales, which is close to current prices. Ending inventory holds old, possibly very stale, costs. **Allowed under US GAAP only; prohibited under IFRS.**
- **Weighted average cost**: every unit carries the same average cost.
- **Specific identification**: used for items that are not interchangeable, such as cars or artworks.

## Worked example (prices rising)

Purchases above, 200 sold at 20 (revenue 4,000):

| Method | Cost of sales | Ending inventory | Gross profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| FIFO | 2,200 | 1,400 | 1,800 |
| Weighted average (12) | 2,400 | 1,200 | 1,600 |
| LIFO | 2,600 | 1,000 | 1,400 |

## Exam traps

- **When prices rise**, LIFO gives the highest cost of sales, lowest profit, lowest taxes, lowest inventory, and so higher inventory turnover. When prices fall, every effect reverses.
- **Total cost of sales + ending inventory = total goods available** (3,600 here) under every method.
- **LIFO's tax saving is real cash**, which is why US companies use it despite lower reported profit.

## Questions people ask

### Which method gives higher profit when prices rise?

FIFO. It charges the oldest, cheapest units to cost of sales, so profit and closing inventory are both higher. LIFO charges the newest, dearest units, lowering profit and tax.

### Is LIFO allowed under IFRS?

No. IFRS permits FIFO, weighted average and specific identification. LIFO is allowed only under US GAAP, mainly because of its tax advantage when prices rise.

### Which balance sheet figure is more realistic?

FIFO's, because closing inventory is made of the most recent purchases. Under LIFO, inventory can be valued at prices that are decades old.
