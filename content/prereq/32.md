> \[MR = MC\]
>
> Produce where marginal revenue equals marginal cost. In the short run, keep going as long as price covers average variable cost.

## From first principles

**Why MR = MC?** If one more unit brings in more than it costs (MR > MC), make it. If it costs more than it brings in (MR < MC), don't. Profit is highest where the two are equal. For a price-taker in a competitive market, MR equals the price, so the rule becomes **P = MC** (on the rising part of the MC curve).

**When is the firm making money?** If price is at least **average total cost**, it covers every cost: the **breakeven** point is price = minimum ATC. Economists count a normal return on the owners' capital as a cost, so "breakeven" means **zero economic profit**, which still includes a normal accounting profit.

**Should a loss-making firm close?** Fixed costs must be paid either way in the short run. So the right question is whether producing covers the **variable** costs:

- Price **above minimum AVC**: producing covers variable costs and pays part of the fixed costs. **Keep operating**, even at a loss.
- Price **below minimum AVC**: every unit loses money on top of the fixed costs. **Shut down** now.
- **Long run**: every cost is variable, so a firm that cannot cover ATC **exits** the industry.

## Worked example 1: a simple decision

Fixed costs are 500 a month; at the best output of 100 units, AVC = 8 and ATC = 13. At a price of 10, revenue is 1,000 and variable costs 800:

\[ \text{Profit if operating} = 1{,}000 - 800 - 500 = -300 \qquad \text{Profit if shut} = -500 \]

**Keep operating**: it loses less. At a price of 7, revenue would not cover variable costs, so it should shut down.

## Worked example 2: using a cost table

Take the firm from [the short-run cost lesson](prereq-short-run-costs.html): fixed cost 100, marginal cost of 40, 30, 25, 30, 40, 50, 60 for units 1 to 7, minimum AVC 31.25 (at 4 units) and minimum ATC 52.5 (at 6 units). It sells in a competitive market at a price of 40.

- **Output**: produce while price ≥ MC, on the rising part of MC. The 5th unit costs 40 and the 6th costs 50, so produce **5 units**.
- **Profit**: revenue 5 × 40 = 200; total cost 265; loss **65**.
- **Decision**: shutting down would lose the fixed cost of 100, so operating is better.

The price thresholds for this firm:

- **Shutdown point**: price = minimum AVC = **31.25**.
- **Breakeven point**: price = minimum ATC = **52.5**.

## Exam traps

- **Breakeven uses ATC; shutdown uses AVC.**
- **Sunk and fixed costs do not change the short-run output decision**; they only decide whether the firm profits.
- **Economic profit counts opportunity costs**, including a normal return to owners; zero economic profit is still a normal accounting profit.
- **In the long run there is no shutdown point**, only exit, because every cost can be avoided.

## Questions people ask

### Why do firms produce where marginal revenue equals marginal cost?

Because every unit before that point adds more to revenue than to cost, and every unit after it adds more to cost than to revenue. Stopping exactly where they are equal leaves profit as high as it can be.

### When should a firm shut down in the short run?

When the price falls below the minimum of average variable cost. Producing would then lose money on every unit on top of the fixed costs that must be paid anyway.

### What is the difference between breakeven and shutdown?

Breakeven is the price at which the firm covers all its costs (minimum ATC), earning zero economic profit. Shutdown is the lower price at which it can no longer even cover its variable costs (minimum AVC).
