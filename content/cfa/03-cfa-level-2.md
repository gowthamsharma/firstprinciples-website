Level II takes the Level I tools and asks you to **apply** them, mostly to valuation: what is this share, bond, derivative or company worth, which model fits, and which assumption drives the answer?

## Format: the item set

Every question belongs to an **item set**: a one- or two-page case study (the *vignette*) followed by four multiple-choice questions that can only be answered from it.

- **22 item sets, 88 questions** in total; 20 sets are scored and 2 are unscored trial sets.
- **4 hours 24 minutes**, in two sessions of **2 hours 12 minutes** with 11 sets each.
- About **12 minutes per set**, or 3 minutes per question, including reading time.
- Exam windows: **May, August and November**.

Because the four questions share one vignette, one misunderstanding can cost all four. That is the main reason Level II feels harder than Level I even though the content is related.

## What this means for how you study

- **Depth over breadth.** Expect multi-step calculations (for example a full free-cash-flow valuation) where an early slip propagates.
- **Revisit Level I foundations.** Level II assumes them. Shaky accounting at Level I becomes a real problem in Level II financial statement analysis.
- **Practise vignette technique, not just content.** Read the questions first, then read the case looking for the inputs they need. See [Tackling Level II item sets](blog-level-2-item-sets.html).
- **Recent pass rates:** 42% (November 2025), 43% (May 2026) and 45% (August 2026), against a five-year average of 46%.


## What each topic tests at Level II

Level II takes most Level I topics from "know the tool" to "use it on a company". The table summarises what each area asks you to do.

| Topic | What you do with it at Level II |
|---|---|
| Quantitative methods | read multiple-regression output, test model assumptions (heteroskedasticity, serial correlation, multicollinearity), build time-series models and judge machine-learning results |
| Economics | price currencies with interest rate parity and forwards, forecast exchange rates, and explain long-run growth with growth accounting |
| Financial statement analysis | consolidate investments in other companies, analyse pensions and share-based pay, translate foreign subsidiaries, assess banks and insurers, and judge earnings quality |
| Corporate issuers | analyse dividends and buybacks, environmental, social and governance factors, cost of capital in practice, and mergers and restructurings |
| Equity valuation | value companies with dividend discount, free cash flow and residual income models, market multiples and private-company methods |
| Fixed income | price bonds with arbitrage-free trees, value embedded options and convertibles, model credit risk and price credit default swaps |
| Derivatives | price and value forwards, futures and swaps, and value options with binomial trees and Black–Scholes–Merton |
| Alternative investments | value real estate (direct and through REITs), private equity and commodities |
| Portfolio management | use multifactor models, measure and manage risk, and apply the fundamental law of active management |
| Ethics | apply the Code and Standards to longer, more ambiguous cases where several Standards may be in play |

## How Level II is scored

- **Every question is worth the same.** A difficult valuation question counts no more than a short definition question in the same set.
- **No negative marking:** answer every question.
- **The pass mark is not published.** CFA Institute sets a minimum passing score after each exam. Most candidates aim for above 70% in mock exams before sitting.
- Your score report shows your performance by topic against other candidates. It is useful if you need to sit again.

## A 300-hour study plan

Most successful candidates study **300 hours or more** for Level II, about **20 hours a week for 15–16 weeks**.

| Phase | Weeks | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Foundations | 1–2 | Refresh Level I accounting, time value of money and bond basics; Level II assumes them |
| Heavy topics | 3–9 | Equity valuation, financial statements, fixed income and derivatives, with item sets at the end of every reading |
| Lighter topics | 10–12 | Quant, economics, corporate issuers, alternatives and portfolio management |
| Ethics | throughout | Two short sessions a week rather than one block at the end |
| Mocks and review | 13–16 | At least three full mocks under timed conditions, with a review day after each |

## Who finds Level II hardest, and why

- **Candidates who passed Level I by memorising.** Level II questions change the setting so that recognising a formula is not enough. You need to know when it applies.
- **Candidates without an accounting background.** Pensions, intercompany investments and foreign currency translation are the readings most likely to be new.
- **Fast readers who skim.** Vignettes hide key inputs in a single sentence, such as a tax rate, a change in policy or a footnote on a lease.

The fix in all three cases is the same: fewer readings at a time, more item sets, and a written note of every mistake you make.

## Questions people ask

### How many questions are on CFA Level 2?

88 multiple-choice questions grouped into 22 item sets, each built around a short case study.

### Which topics carry the most weight at Level 2?

Financial statements, equities, fixed income, portfolio construction and ethics each carry the higher ranges. The other topics carry lower ranges, but every topic is tested.

### When can I sit Level 2?

It is offered in three windows a year. Check the current calendar for exact dates and registration deadlines.

### How is Level 2 different from Level 1?

Fewer topics are tested in less breadth but much more depth, and every question is tied to a case, so applying ideas matters more than recalling them.


### How many hours should I study for Level 2?

Plan for at least 300 hours. Candidates without a finance or accounting background often need 350 or more, especially for financial statement analysis and equity valuation.

### Is there a formula sheet for Level 2?

No sheet is given in the exam. Our [Level II formula sheet](blog-cfa-level-2-formula-sheet.html) collects the formulas by topic so you can learn them.
