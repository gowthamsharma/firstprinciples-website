## Sixty years in four eras

**1963–1979: a small club of experienced analysts.** The first exam, on 15 June 1963, was sat by 284 working investment professionals at 25 locations in the United States; 268 passed. Through the 1960s and 1970s fewer than 2,500 people sat in a year, and pass rates averaged about 70% at Level I, 77% at Level II and 80% at Level III. These were senior practitioners formalising what they already did.

**1980–1999: growth and a rising bar.** Candidate numbers grew from about 2,000 in 1980 to 45,000 in 1999, as the charter spread outside North America (Singapore formed the first society outside North America in 1987). Average pass rates fell to about 57% at Level I, 59% at Level II and 67% at Level III.

**2000–2019: the global boom.** Candidates passed 100,000 a year in 2007 and peaked at **270,456 in 2019**, across a June exam for all levels and a December Level I exam (added in 2003). With far more early-career candidates, pass rates settled at today's levels: about 41% at Level I, 45% at Level II and 56% at Level III.

**2020 onwards: pandemic, computers and recovery.** The June 2020 exams were cancelled. In 2021 the exams moved from paper in exam halls to computers in test centres, with several smaller windows a year. Many candidates had deferred more than once, and results dropped sharply: **Level I fell to 22% in July 2021**, the lowest in the program's history, and Level II to 29% in August 2021. Since 2022 pass rates have recovered to 36–46% at Level I, 39–59% at Level II and 47–50% at Level III.

## First-time candidates do much better

The headline rate mixes everyone who sat. CFA Institute also reports how first-time candidates did, and the gap is large:

- **Level I, August 2026:** 49% of first-time candidates passed, against 29% of those who had deferred at least once.
- **Level III, August 2026:** 56% of first-time candidates passed.

Deferring is sometimes the right call, but the numbers say that candidates who sit when scheduled, after steady preparation, pass far more often. The deferral group also includes people who were short of time for their first date, which explains part of the gap.

## What your chances really are

- **At each level, fewer than half pass.** Over the last ten years, about 40% at Level I, 45% at Level II and 50% at Level III.
- **The chain is long.** If you multiply the ten-year averages (0.40 × 0.45 × 0.50), only about 9% of candidates would pass all three levels at the first attempt *if* each exam were a coin toss. They are not: a well-prepared candidate's chances are much higher at every level, which is why first-time rates are higher. But the arithmetic explains why most charterholders needed a retake somewhere.
- **Level III's higher rate does not make it easier.** Everyone sitting it has already passed two exams.
- **Only people who sat are counted.** Candidates who registered and did not turn up are not in the denominator.

## Is there a minimum passing score?

CFA Institute does not publish one. Its board sets the passing standard after each exam, using judgment from experienced charterholders about what a minimally competent candidate should know. The practical lesson is to aim well above any rumoured line. A common target is to score 70% or more on full mock exams in the final weeks.

## Questions people ask

### What is the CFA Level 1 pass rate?

43% in August 2026, 39% in May 2026 and 45% in February 2026. The 10-year average is 40%, and 49% of first-time candidates passed in August 2026.

### What was the lowest CFA pass rate ever?

Level I in July 2021, at 22%, during the first year of computer-based testing, when many candidates had deferred repeatedly through the pandemic.

### Why were CFA pass rates so high in the 1960s?

The early candidates were experienced investment professionals, and there were only a few hundred of them a year. As the program grew to hundreds of thousands of mostly early-career candidates, pass rates fell to today's levels.

### Have CFA pass rates gone up or down recently?

Up from the 2021 lows. Since 2022 Level I has ranged from 36% to 46%, Level II from 39% to 59% and Level III from 47% to 50%, close to the 10-year averages.

### How many people have passed CFA Level III?

357,002 Level III passes have been recorded since 1963. Not all became charterholders, because the charter also needs work experience and membership; CFA Institute reports more than 200,000 charterholders today.
