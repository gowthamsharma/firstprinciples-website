Concept 4 of 50  /  Financial Statement Analysis

Revenue recognition in five steps

Revenue is recognised when a promised good or service is delivered to the customer, at the price allocated to that promise.

2 min read  /  By Gowtham Kumar

Find each promise in the contract, put a price on it, and count the revenue when that promise is kept.

From first principles

A customer pays because the seller promises something. Revenue therefore belongs to the moment each promise is kept, not the moment the contract is signed or the cash arrives. IFRS and US GAAP converged on the same five-step model:

  1. Identify the contract with the customer: an agreement that creates enforceable rights and obligations.
  2. Identify the performance obligations: each distinct good or service promised.
  3. Determine the transaction price: what the seller expects to receive, including estimates of variable amounts such as rebates or bonuses (only to the extent a significant reversal is unlikely).
  4. Allocate the price to each obligation in proportion to its stand-alone selling price.
  5. Recognise revenue when (or as) each obligation is satisfied, which means when control passes to the customer, either at a point in time or over time.

Worked example

A phone sold with a two-year service plan for a single price of 1,000. Stand-alone prices: phone 600, service 600 (total 1,200). Allocation: the phone gets 1,000 × 600/1,200 = 500, recognised at delivery; the service gets 500, recognised evenly at about 20.8 a month for 24 months.

Exam traps

  • Bundles are split: one price, several obligations, several timings.
  • Variable consideration is constrained: only include it if a significant reversal is not highly likely.
  • Principal or agent? An agent (for example, a marketplace) reports only its commission as revenue, not the gross sale.
  • Long-term contracts recognised over time use a measure of progress, such as costs incurred to date over total expected costs.

Questions people ask

What are the five steps of revenue recognition?

Find the contract, list the separate promises in it, set the total price, split that price across the promises by their stand-alone selling prices, and book each slice as the promise is fulfilled, at a point in time or over time.

When is revenue recognised over time rather than at a point?

When the customer gets the benefit as the work proceeds, controls the asset while it is being built, or the seller builds something with no alternative use and has a right to be paid for work done so far. Long construction contracts are the typical case.

How do analysts spot aggressive revenue recognition?

Watch receivables growing faster than sales, unusual end-of-quarter spikes, changes in estimates of contract progress and a rise in unbilled revenue. Each means revenue is running ahead of cash.
