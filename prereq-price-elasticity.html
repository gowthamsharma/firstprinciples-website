Price elasticity of demand and total revenue

By Gowtham KumarLesson 27 of 50

Elasticity measures how strongly buyers react to a price change. It decides whether a price rise raises or lowers a seller's revenue, and it changes along a straight-line demand curve.

E=%ΔQ%ΔPE = \frac{\%\Delta Q}{\%\Delta P}E=%ΔP%ΔQ​

Above 1 (in absolute value), buyers react strongly.

From first principles

When a price rises, people buy less. The question for a seller is how much less. If quantity falls by a larger percentage than price rises, revenue falls; if by a smaller percentage, revenue rises.

That ratio of percentages is the price elasticity of demand. It is negative (price and quantity move in opposite directions), so we usually talk about its absolute value:

  • |E| > 1: elastic. Buyers are sensitive. A price cut raises revenue.
  • |E| < 1: inelastic. Buyers barely react. A price rise raises revenue.
  • |E| = 1: unit elastic. Revenue is at its maximum.

Because revenue is P×QP \times QP×Q, a 1% price rise changes revenue by roughly 1%+E%1\% + E\%1%+E%. With E = −0.4, revenue rises about 0.6%; with E = −2, it falls about 1%.

Measuring it

At a single point on a demand curve (point elasticity):

E=ΔQΔP×PQE = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} \times \frac{P}{Q}E=ΔPΔQ​×QP​

Between two points (arc elasticity), use the midpoints as the base, so the answer is the same whichever direction you move:

E=(Q2−Q1)/12(Q1+Q2)(P2−P1)/12(P1+P2)E = \frac{(Q_2 - Q_1) / \tfrac{1}{2}(Q_1 + Q_2)}{(P_2 - P_1) / \tfrac{1}{2}(P_1 + P_2)}E=(P2​−P1​)/21​(P1​+P2​)(Q2​−Q1​)/21​(Q1​+Q2​)​

On a straight-line demand curve, elasticity is not constant: it is high at high prices (where a 1-unit price change is a small percentage of a big number and a large percentage of a small quantity) and low at low prices. Drag the price:

What makes demand elastic

Close substitutes, a large share of the budget, a long time to adjust, and a narrowly defined good (one brand of coffee rather than coffee in general) all make demand more elastic. Petrol in the short run, salt and prescription medicines are classic inelastic goods.

Exam traps

  • Slope is not elasticity. A straight line has one slope but every elasticity from zero to infinity.
  • Use the coefficient times P/Q for a demand function; the coefficient alone (−15) is the slope.
  • Perfectly inelastic demand is a vertical line (quantity never changes); perfectly elastic is horizontal.
  • Revenue is maximised where elasticity is −1, which is the midpoint of a straight-line demand curve.

Questions people ask

What does elastic demand mean?

That buyers react strongly to price: the percentage change in quantity is larger than the percentage change in price. Cutting the price then raises total revenue, and raising it lowers revenue.

Why does elasticity change along a straight-line demand curve?

The slope is constant, but the ratio of price to quantity is not. At high prices and low quantities, a given change is a big percentage of quantity and a small percentage of price, so demand is elastic; at low prices the reverse holds.

How do you calculate elasticity from a demand equation?

Multiply the coefficient on price by the ratio of price to quantity at the point you care about: E = (ΔQ/ΔP) × (P/Q). Calculate quantity first by plugging all the given values into the equation.

Income and cross-price elasticity

How demand reacts to income separates luxuries, necessities and inferior goods. How it reacts to another good's price separates substitutes from complements. Both come straight out of a demand equation.

Substitution and income effects

A price change does two things at once: it makes a good relatively cheaper, and it changes how rich the buyer feels. Separating the two explains normal, inferior and Giffen goods, and why Veblen goods are a different story.

Marginal returns and productivity

With some inputs fixed, adding more of a variable input eventually adds less and less output. That single fact shapes every short-run cost curve. In the long run, when every input can change, the question becomes returns to scale.
