Analyst adjustments for comparability

Reported numbers follow each company's choices and standards. Before comparing companies, put them on the same basis.

2 min read  /  By Gowtham Kumar

Compare like with like: undo accounting choices before comparing economics.

From first principles

Two companies with identical businesses can report very different numbers because of their accounting standard (IFRS or US GAAP) and their choices within it. A screen or ratio comparison on raw numbers mostly measures those choices. Analysts therefore adjust:

  • Inventory: convert LIFO to FIFO using the LIFO reserve (inventory, cost of sales, equity, deferred tax).
  • Investments: put securities held in different measurement categories (fair value through profit, through OCI, or amortised cost) on one basis.
  • Goodwill: remove it from assets and equity to compare tangible book value and returns, especially across companies that grew by acquisition versus organically.
  • PP&E: compare depreciation policies, and estimate average age (accumulated depreciation / depreciation expense) to anticipate future capital spending.
  • Off-balance-sheet obligations: add debt-like items before comparing leverage.

Worked example

Company A (organic growth) has equity of 800 and no goodwill. Company B (grew by acquisitions) has equity of 1,000 including goodwill of 300. Net income is 120 for each. Reported ROE: A 15%, B 12%. Tangible ROE: A 15%, B 120 / 700 = 17.1%. B's underlying business may be more profitable; it simply paid premiums for acquisitions.

Exam traps

  • Adjust both the balance sheet and the related income statement items, or ratios mix bases.
  • Do not over-adjust: goodwill is a real cost of growth to the shareholders who paid for it.

Questions people ask

Why do analysts adjust reported numbers?

To compare companies on the same basis. Different accounting choices, such as LIFO versus FIFO or capitalising versus expensing, can make similar businesses look different.

What are the most common adjustments?

Converting LIFO to FIFO, removing goodwill or one-off items, adding off-balance-sheet obligations to debt and putting capitalised versus expensed costs on the same footing.

Is there one right set of adjustments?

No. The right adjustment depends on the question. For credit analysis you add hidden debts; for valuation you focus on recurring earnings and economic assets.
