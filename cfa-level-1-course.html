CFA Level I course: all ten topics, built from first principles

By Gowtham KumarThe course

The First Principles CFA Level I course: what each of the ten topics covers under the 2027 outline, how many study hours to give it, how the lessons work, and the free Level I material you can use today.

Level I is about breadth: 180 multiple-choice questions across ten topics, about 90 seconds each. The course is built to make that breadth manageable by showing how few ideas sit underneath it: time value, the accounting equation, no-arbitrage, and the trade-off between risk and return.

The ten topics and how long to give each

The hours below share a 300-hour plan in proportion to the midpoint of each 2027 topic weight. Adjust them to your background: an accountant can move hours from financial statements to quant; an engineer the other way.

Leave the last four to six weeks for full mock exams and review; the study plan guide and the free Excel planner build the calendar for you.

How each lesson works

  1. A question first. "Why does a bond's price fall when rates rise?" rather than "Learning outcome 4b".
  2. The picture. An animated diagram builds the idea one step at a time.
  3. The formula, named last. By the time it appears, it only writes down what you have already seen.
  4. Pause and predict. You commit to an answer before the lesson shows it.
  5. Exam questions. Three-option questions in the Level I format, with the reasoning behind every wrong option.

The order we recommend

Quant and economics first, because they supply the tools; then financial statements, because equity and fixed income valuation use them; then corporate issuers, equity, fixed income, derivatives, alternatives and portfolio management; ethics early and again at the end. Questions people ask

How many hours do I need for CFA Level I?

Candidates who pass typically report about 300 hours, spread over five to six months. The table above shares those hours across the topics by weight.

Which Level I topics should I study first?

Quantitative methods and economics, then financial statement analysis, because later topics use their tools. Keep ethics going throughout: it is short to read but needs repeated practice.

Is there a free CFA Level I course?

The prerequisites course, 100 practice questions, the calculator guide, formula sheet and study planner on this site are free. CFA Institute's own curriculum and practice questions are included with your exam registration.
