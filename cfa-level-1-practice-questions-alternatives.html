CFA Level I practice questions: Alternative investments, with worked answers

By Gowtham KumarPractice questions

Ten original alternative investments questions in the Level I format, three options each, with an explanation for every answer. Hedge funds, private equity, real estate, infrastructure, commodities and their fees. Alternative investments carries 6–9% of the exam from 2027.

Alternative investments covers hedge funds, private equity, real estate, infrastructure, commodities and digital assets. The favourite calculation is a fee waterfall; the favourite concept is why reported returns of illiquid assets look smoother than they are.

Topic weight from 2027: 6–9% of Level I, about 11 to 16 of the 180 questions.

10 questions · Level I format · about 15 minutes

Choose an answer to see the explanation.

  1. A hedge fund charges a 2% management fee on starting assets and a 20% incentive fee on gains after the management fee. Assets start at 100 million and earn a gross return of 15%. What is the investor's net return?

    Answer: A. Management fee = 2% × 100 = 2.0. Gain after it = 15 − 2 = 13, so the incentive fee = 20% × 13 = 2.6. Total fees = 4.6, and the net return = 15 − 4.6 = 10.4%. Fee terms vary: always check whether the incentive is calculated before or after the management fee.

  2. A building produces net operating income of 600,000 a year. Similar buildings sell at a capitalisation rate of 6%. What is it worth?

    Answer: C. Value = NOI / cap rate = 600,000 / 0.06 = 10 million. A lower cap rate means buyers pay more for each unit of income.

  3. Why do private equity funds typically show negative returns in their first few years (the J-curve)?

    Answer: C. Capital is called gradually and fees start immediately, while investments are held at cost and value is created over several years. Returns turn positive as companies grow and are sold.

  4. Futures prices for a commodity rise with maturity (contango). For an investor who holds long futures and rolls them over, the roll return is most likely:

    Answer: B. In contango the investor sells the expiring, cheaper contract and buys the next, dearer one. As each contract converges to spot, the position loses value: a negative roll yield.

  5. A brownfield infrastructure investment, compared with a greenfield one, usually involves:

    Answer: A. Brownfield assets already operate, such as a toll road with years of traffic data, so cash flows are more predictable. Greenfield projects carry construction and demand risk.

  6. A hedge fund has a high-water mark. This means it:

    Answer: C. If the fund falls from 120 to 100, it must first climb back above 120 before it earns another incentive fee. This stops managers being paid twice for recovering the same ground.

  7. Why do appraisal-based returns for real estate and private equity often understate true risk?

    Answer: C. Appraisers anchor to past values and update slowly, so reported returns look smoother than the underlying economics. Analysts often unsmooth the series before comparing risk with listed assets.

  8. Compared with owning a building directly, investing in a listed REIT gives:

    Answer: C. REIT shares trade daily, small amounts buy a slice of many properties, and a professional team manages them. In exchange, prices respond to the equity market in the short run.

  9. A main drawback of a fund of hedge funds is that:

    Answer: C. Funds of funds provide manager selection, diversification and access, but they charge their own management (and sometimes incentive) fees on top of the hedge funds' fees.

  10. The total return on a fully collateralised long commodity futures position comes from:

    Answer: C. Investors earn the change in the spot price, the gain or loss from rolling contracts (roll yield) and interest on the cash held as collateral.

Three things to remember for alternative investments

  • Read fee questions twice: is the incentive fee charged on gross returns or after the management fee? Is there a hurdle or a high-water mark?
  • Value = NOI / cap rate.
  • Commodity futures returns = spot + roll + collateral.

How to use these questions

Answer each question before you look at the explanation, and time yourself: the real exam allows about 90 seconds per question. Mark every question you got right for the wrong reason as wrong. When you miss one, write down the idea behind it rather than the answer, and come back to the page in a week.

These questions are written by First Principles to match the style and level of the exam. They are not taken from CFA Institute's curriculum or mock exams, which you should also use: they are included with your exam registration.

No. CFA Institute does not publish past exam questions. These are original questions written to match the Level I format: a short stem and three answer options, testing one idea each.
