CFA Level I practice questions: Fixed income, with worked answers

By Gowtham KumarPractice questions

Ten original fixed income questions in the Level I format, three options each, with an explanation for every answer. Bond pricing, yields, the term structure, duration, convexity and credit. Fixed income carries 11–14% of the exam from 2027.

Fixed income is where many Level I candidates lose marks: bond pricing, yield measures, the term structure, duration and convexity, and credit. Almost every concept follows from one idea: a bond's price is the present value of its cash flows.

Topic weight from 2027: 11–14% of Level I, about 20 to 25 of the 180 questions.

10 questions · Level I format · about 15 minutes

Choose an answer to see the explanation.

  1. A three-year bond pays an annual coupon of 5% and yields 6% to maturity. What is its price per 100 of face value?

    Answer: A. Price = 5/1.06 + 5/1.06² + 105/1.06³ = 97.33. The coupon is below the market yield, so the bond trades at a discount to face value.

  2. A bond trades above its face value. Which statement is correct?

    Answer: C. A premium price means investors pay more today because the bond's coupons are generous compared with current yields. Its price will drift down to face value as maturity approaches.

  3. A bond has a modified duration of 7. If its yield rises by 50 basis points, its price will change by about:

    Answer: B. Percentage price change ≈ −modified duration × change in yield = −7 × 0.005 = −3.5%. Prices fall when yields rise.

  4. A bond has a modified duration of 7 and convexity of 60. Yields rise by 1 percentage point. What is the estimated price change?

    Answer: B. −7 × 0.01 + ½ × 60 × 0.01² = −7.0% + 0.3% = −6.7%. Positive convexity cushions the loss when yields rise and adds to the gain when they fall.

  5. A bond with a 6% coupon (on 100 face) trades at 95. What is its current yield?

    Answer: C. Current yield = annual coupon / price = 6 / 95 = 6.32%. It ignores the gain from the price rising to 100 at maturity, so it is not the yield to maturity.

  6. The one-year spot rate is 3% and the two-year spot rate is 4%. What is the one-year rate one year from now implied by these rates (the 1y1y forward)?

    Answer: B. Investing for two years at 4% must equal one year at 3% then one year at the forward rate: 1.04² = 1.03 × (1 + f), so f = 1.0816 / 1.03 − 1 = 5.01%.

  7. A bond has a 2% annual probability of default and an expected recovery of 40% of its value. What is its expected annual loss?

    Answer: A. Expected loss = probability of default × loss given default = 2% × (1 − 40%) = 1.2%. 0.8% uses the recovery rate instead of the loss.

  8. Compared with an otherwise identical bond without the option, a callable bond:

    Answer: B. The issuer holds the call option, so the investor pays less: callable = straight bond − call. When yields fall the issuer is more likely to call, capping the price (negative convexity).

  9. A company defaults. In what order are these creditors paid?

    Answer: A. Secured creditors are paid first from the assets pledged to them, then senior unsecured, then subordinated debt; shareholders come last. In practice negotiated settlements sometimes deviate from strict priority.

  10. A five-year zero-coupon bond yields 4% a year (annual compounding). What is its price per 100 of face value?

    Answer: A. Price = 100 / 1.04⁵ = 82.19. 80.00 subtracts 5 × 4% without compounding; 96.15 discounts for one year only.

Three things to remember for fixed income

  • If the coupon is below the yield, the bond trades below par, and vice versa.
  • Duration gives the first estimate of the price change; convexity corrects it, always upwards for option-free bonds.
  • Forward rates make investing short and rolling equal to investing long.

How to use these questions

Answer each question before you look at the explanation, and time yourself: the real exam allows about 90 seconds per question. Mark every question you got right for the wrong reason as wrong. When you miss one, write down the idea behind it rather than the answer, and come back to the page in a week.

These questions are written by First Principles to match the style and level of the exam. They are not taken from CFA Institute's curriculum or mock exams, which you should also use: they are included with your exam registration.

Questions people ask

No. CFA Institute does not publish past exam questions. These are original questions written to match the Level I format: a short stem and three answer options, testing one idea each.
