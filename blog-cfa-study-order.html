The best order to study CFA topics at Level I, II and III

By Gowtham KumarStudy blog

Study the topics that others build on first. At Level I that means quant and economics before financial statements, and financial statements before equity and fixed income. Here are tested orders for all three levels, and the reasons behind them.

The CFA curriculum is written so that later topics use earlier ones. Study them in the right order and each topic is easier; study them in the wrong order and you keep meeting tools you have not learned yet. The rule is simple: foundations first, applications next, synthesis last, with ethics spread across the whole plan.

Level I

Order Topic Why here
1 Ethics (first pass) Sets the rules you will apply everywhere; revisit it monthly
2 Quantitative methods Time value, statistics and the calculator, used in almost every later topic
3 Economics Interest rates, inflation and currencies feed fixed income and equity
4 Financial statement analysis The largest building block; needed for corporate issuers and equity
5 Corporate issuers Uses accounting and time value: capital structure, cost of capital, investment decisions
6 Equity investments Uses accounting and discounting to value companies
7 Fixed income Uses time value and economics; bond pricing, yields, duration
8 Derivatives Builds on fixed income and time value
9 Alternative investments Uses ideas from equity, fixed income and derivatives
10 Portfolio management Brings everything together: risk, return and diversification
— Ethics (second pass) In the final month, with lots of questions

Level II

Order Topic Why here
1 Quantitative methods Regression, needed to read research output in other vignettes
2 Economics Currency and growth models used in valuation
3 Financial statement analysis Adjustments you need before valuing anything
4 Corporate issuers Capital structure and corporate decisions
5 Equity valuation Applies the accounting and corporate topics
6 Fixed income Term structure, credit and valuation trees
7 Derivatives Builds on fixed income pricing
8 Alternative investments Real estate, private equity, commodities
9 Portfolio management Multifactor models, risk and active management
10 Ethics Heavy question practice near the end, with a first pass at the start

Level III

Order Topic Why here
1 Asset allocation The framework for every client case
2 Portfolio construction Equity, fixed income and alternatives strategies inside that framework
3 Derivatives and risk management Tools to adjust and hedge the portfolios you have built
4 Performance measurement How you judge the results
5 Your pathway Builds on all of the above; the largest single block
6 Ethics Applied to portfolio management, practised throughout

At Level III, start practising written answers from the first topic, not only at the end; the format is the hardest part.

Three rules that matter more than the exact order

  1. Revise as you go. Spend 20% of each week on topics you finished earlier, or they fade by exam day.
  2. Questions every week, from the first topic. Reading without practice gives a false sense of progress.
  3. Leave six weeks at the end for mock exams and review, whatever order you choose.

Our free Excel study planner follows these orders automatically: choose your level and dates, and it assigns a focus topic to every week.

Questions people ask

Which CFA Level I topic should I study first?

Quantitative methods (with a first pass of ethics), because time value and statistics are used in almost every other topic.

Should I study ethics first or last?

Both. Read it early so you know the rules, then practise it heavily in the final month, when it counts most.

Should I follow the curriculum order?

The curriculum order is close to the dependency order above. If your materials use a different order, follow the logic: foundations before the topics that use them.
