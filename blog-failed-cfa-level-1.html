Failed CFA Level I? What to do next, step by step

By Gowtham KumarStudy blog

More than half of Level I candidates fail each session, so you are in the majority, not an exception. How to read your score report, when you can sit again under the attempt rules, what to change in your method, and a 20-week plan for the retake.

In November 2025, 57% of Level I candidates did not pass. In every Level I session since 2022, between 54% and 65% of candidates failed. Failing Level I is the most common outcome of a first attempt, and it says much more about method and time than about ability. Here is how to turn it into a pass.

Step 1: take a week off, then read the score report properly

Your score report shows two things: your overall position against the minimum passing score, and a bar for each topic. Do not try to read a percentage into it; CFA Institute does not publish the pass mark. Read it like this:

  • How far from the line were you? Close to the line means your method mostly worked and needs sharpening. Far below means the plan itself needs changing: usually not enough hours, or too little practice.
  • Which heavy topics were weak? A weak bar in a topic worth 11–14% (ethics, quant, financial statements, equities, fixed income) costs far more than a weak bar in a 6–9% topic.
  • Was ethics weak? It often is for people who fail narrowly, because it is easy to under-practise.

Step 2: know the rules for sitting again

  • You may take each level up to six times in total.
  • You may take an exam at most twice in a calendar year, and not in consecutive windows or in windows less than six months apart.
  • If you sat Level I in November 2026, February 2027 is out (the next window). In practice the earliest realistic retake is May 2027 (standard registration closes on 10 February 2027) or August 2027, where early registration at the lower fee is open until 20 January 2027. The registration system shows only windows you are eligible for.
  • From February 2027 the paid deferral option ends: miss your exam and you lose the fee. Pick a window you can really prepare for.

Our exam dates and fees page lists every 2027 window and deadline.

Step 3: change the method, not just the effort

Most candidates who fail studied hard. What usually went wrong is one of these:

  1. Too much reading, too little practice. Aim to spend at least half of your hours on questions, and to finish the curriculum with six weeks left for mocks.
  2. Topics "covered" but not retained. You read fixed income in March and met it again in the exam in August. Mixed question sets, done every week, keep old topics alive.
  3. No error log. Write down every question you got wrong, the idea behind it and why you missed it. Re-read the log weekly; most errors repeat.
  4. Mocks too late. Take your first full mock about six weeks out, under exam timing, and review it for longer than it took to sit.
  5. Memorised formulas. If you cannot explain why a formula holds, the exam's twist on it will catch you. That is the gap our first-principles method is built to close.

Step 4: a 20-week retake plan

Weeks What to do Hours a week
1–2 Score report diagnosis; error log from memory; rebuild the plan 6
3–10 Re-learn the weak heavy topics, one or two a week, with questions every day 12–15
11–14 Mixed question sets across all ten topics; ethics twice a week 12–15
15–19 Four to six full mocks, each followed by a full review 15–20
20 Light review, formula sheet, ethics cases, exam-day logistics 8

That is roughly 250 hours: a little less than a first attempt, because you are not starting from zero. Our free Excel study planner builds this week by week from your exam date.

Step 5: test the weak topics now

Before you plan anything, try ten questions in your weakest topic and see how it feels today:

  • Ethics · Quantitative methods · Financial statements · Fixed income · Equity
  • All ten topics, 100 questions

What failing does not mean

It does not mean you are not suited to the work. Fewer than one candidate in ten passes all three levels at the first attempt, and at average pass rates a typical candidate needs six or seven exam attempts to clear the three levels (our simulation). The charter is valued precisely because almost everyone who holds it failed or struggled somewhere on the way.

Questions people ask

When can I retake CFA Level I after failing in November?

Not in the next window (February), and not in a window less than six months after your attempt. For a November 2026 attempt, that usually means May 2027 at the earliest; August 2027 gives a fuller study cycle and is still open for early registration until 20 January 2027.

How many times can you take CFA Level I?

Up to six times in total, at most twice in a calendar year.

Should I use different study materials for my retake?

Keep the materials that worked, but change something about how you practise: a new question bank or mock set, an error log, and mocks starting six weeks out. Re-reading the same notes rarely closes the gap.

Does failing Level I hurt my career?

Employers rarely ask about failed attempts. What they see is progress: "passed Level I" or "Level II candidate". Many charterholders failed at least one level.
