Why is the CFA charter so prestigious?

By Gowtham KumarStudy blog

The charter is respected because it is hard to earn, broad, tied to an ethics code that holders reaffirm every year, and recognised at master's-degree level in twelve major markets. Here is the evidence, and the limits of what the charter can do for you.

People in investment management often call the CFA charter the industry's gold standard. That reputation was not granted; it was earned over sixty years, and it rests on things you can check.

1. It is hard to earn

  • Fewer than half pass any level. Over the last ten years, about 40% passed Level I, 45% Level II and 50% Level III (every session since 1963).
  • It takes years. Candidates typically study 300 hours or more per level, and most take two to four years to pass all three exams (how long it really takes).
  • It needs experience as well as exams. The charter is awarded only after 4,000 hours of relevant work over at least 36 months.

Scarcity alone does not create respect, but a credential that most people who attempt it do not finish tells an employer something about the people who do.

2. It is broad and deep

The curriculum covers ten topic areas, from ethics, statistics and economics to financial statement analysis, equity, fixed income, derivatives, alternative investments and portfolio management. Level I tests the tools, Level II tests valuation with them, and Level III tests whether you can put a portfolio together for a real client. A charterholder can follow a credit analyst, a portfolio manager and a risk manager in the same meeting, which few degrees guarantee.

3. Ethics is part of the deal

Ethics is examined at every level, and it is not a formality: in close results, it is often what separates a pass from a fail. After the exams, every charterholder completes a Professional Conduct Statement when joining and again every year on renewing membership, and can be disciplined, and lose the right to use the designation, for breaking the Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct. Clients and employers know the letters come with obligations.

4. Independent bodies rank it at master's level

In 2022 Ecctis, the UK agency that compares qualifications internationally, found the CFA charter comparable to master's-degree standard in all twelve markets it studied: the US, Canada, Brazil, the UK, France, South Africa, India, China, Hong Kong, Singapore, Australia and Japan. In the UK framework the charter and Level III sit at Level 7 (master's), Level II at Level 6 and Level I at Level 5. Ecctis is careful to add that the charter is not an academic degree, but the comparison shows the depth of what is tested.

Several financial regulators also recognise the program, for example in Australia, South Africa and Malaysia, sometimes by waiving parts of their own licensing exams for charterholders.

5. The largest employers hire charterholders in thousands

CFA Institute's 2025 list of the employers with the most charterholders is led by JPMorgan Chase (2,582), RBC (2,532), UBS (2,159), Bank of America (1,680) and Morgan Stanley (1,640), followed by TD Bank, HSBC, BlackRock, Citigroup and Goldman Sachs. Many firms list the charter as preferred in job adverts for research, portfolio management and risk roles, and many pay for their employees' exams.

6. Sixty years of history and a global network

The first charters were awarded in 1963, so the designation has a long track record (the history of the CFA). Today there are more than 200,000 charterholders in over 160 markets and 157 local CFA societies, which run events, job boards and continuing education. In many cities the local society is one of the best professional networks in finance.

What the charter does not do

Prestige has limits, and it is better to know them before you spend three years on it:

  • It is not a licence. In most countries you still need the regulator's own exams or registration to advise clients or trade.
  • It does not replace experience. Employers value it most alongside real work; a charter without relevant experience opens fewer doors than people expect.
  • Its weight depends on the job. It carries most weight in investment research, portfolio management, wealth management and risk. In investment banking deal work, networks and internships often matter more, and in audit or tax, CPA, ACCA or a national accounting qualification is the stronger signal (CFA vs CPA, CFP and ACCA).

Questions people ask

Is the CFA prestigious?

Yes, in investment management it is widely seen as the most respected credential: it is hard to earn, broad, tied to an annually reaffirmed ethics code, and benchmarked at master's level in twelve major markets.

Is the CFA equivalent to a master's degree?

Ecctis found it comparable to master's-degree standard in twelve markets, but it is not an academic degree and does not by itself give entry to further academic study.

Which companies employ the most CFA charterholders?

In CFA Institute's 2025 list: JPMorgan Chase, RBC, UBS, Bank of America and Morgan Stanley, each with more than 1,600 charterholders.

Is the CFA worth it if it is so hard?

For a career in investments, usually yes; see is the CFA worth it and the money side.
