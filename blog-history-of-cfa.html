The history of the CFA charter, from a 1945 proposal to 200,000 charterholders

By Gowtham KumarStudy blog

The CFA charter began as an argument in the first issue of an analysts' journal in 1945. The first exam was sat by 284 people in 1963; by 2019 more than a quarter of a million exams were sat in one year. How a small American credential became the global standard for investment professionals.

Today the CFA charter is held by more than 200,000 people in over 160 markets. It began with a simple question asked in the 1940s: should people who analyse securities for a living have to prove that they are competent, the way doctors, lawyers and accountants do?

1942–1947: the idea

In 1942 a committee of the New York Society of Security Analysts proposed a professional title for security analysts. In January 1945 Benjamin Graham, the investor and teacher now known as the father of value investing, made the case for it in the first issue of the journal that became the Financial Analysts Journal. His arguments were practical: a title would reassure investors that they were dealing with qualified professionals, reward the analysts who earned it, and build a shared sense of professional responsibility.

The idea needed an organisation behind it. On 11 June 1947, the analyst societies of Boston, Chicago, New York and Philadelphia formed the National Federation of Financial Analysts Societies, the forerunner of today's CFA Institute.

1959–1963: the charter is born

The federation's board approved a separate body to award the designation in 1959, and the Institute of Chartered Financial Analysts was incorporated in 1962, together with the CFA designation and a code of conduct.

The first exam was held on 15 June 1963. It was sat by 284 professional investors at 25 locations across the United States; one candidate took it on board the ocean liner Queen Mary on the way to England. Three months later, 268 of them became the first CFA charterholders. Charter number 1 went to George M. Hansen, a former president of the federation. Jane Ashby, one of only six women who sat, was among the first charterholders.

That first exam focused on equity security analysis and on ethical and professional standards, two themes that have stayed at the heart of the program ever since. In 1964 all three levels were offered for the first time, to 1,732 candidates.

1969–1999: standards and growth

  • 1969: the first edition of the Standards of Professional Conduct, the rules every candidate and charterholder still agrees to follow.
  • 1987: Singapore forms the first CFA member society outside North America.
  • 1990: the Association for Investment Management and Research (AIMR) is created as an umbrella over the institute and the analysts' federation; the two fully merge into AIMR in 1999.
  • 1997 and 2001: the first offices outside North America open, in Hong Kong and then London.

The curriculum grew with the industry. CFA Institute's own summary of what was added each decade reads like a history of modern finance: the capital asset pricing model and the Sharpe ratio in the 1960s, Black–Scholes option pricing and mean–variance optimisation in the 1970s, performance attribution and mortgage-backed securities in the 1980s, exchange-traded funds and value at risk in the 1990s.

Candidate numbers rose from about 2,000 a year in 1980 to 45,000 in 1999.

2000–2019: a global standard

In 2003 a December Level I exam was added to the June exam, and in 2004 AIMR renamed itself CFA Institute, after the credential that had become its best-known product. Growth came mostly from outside the United States, especially Asia. In 2019 a record 270,456 exams were sat across the June and December sessions.

2020 onwards: pandemic, computers and pathways

The June 2020 exams were cancelled during the COVID-19 pandemic. From 2021 every level moved from paper exams in large halls to computer-based testing in test centres, with several exam windows a year. The first year was hard: many candidates had deferred more than once, and Level I fell to a 22% pass rate in July 2021, the lowest in the program's history (see pass rates, session by session).

In 2023, the program's 60th year, CFA Institute announced a set of changes: Practical Skills Modules (for example in financial modelling and Python) that every candidate completes at each level, and three specialised Level III pathways: portfolio management, private wealth and private markets. The pathways have been examined since 2025.

What has stayed the same

Sixty years on, three things have not changed:

  1. Three levels, in order. Each exam must be passed before the next.
  2. Ethics first. The Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct are examined at every level, and charterholders reaffirm them every year when they renew membership.
  3. Experience as well as exams. The charter is awarded only to people who also have relevant work experience (today, 4,000 hours over at least 36 months).

That combination of exams, experience and an ethics commitment is a large part of why the charter carries weight.

Questions people ask

Who created the CFA?

The idea was championed by Benjamin Graham and the New York Society of Security Analysts in the 1940s. The designation itself was created by the Institute of Chartered Financial Analysts, incorporated in 1962.

When was the first CFA exam?

15 June 1963. 284 candidates sat it and 268 were awarded the charter.

How old is the CFA Program?

The first exam was held in 1963, so the program passed its 60th anniversary in 2023.

When did AIMR become CFA Institute?

In 2004. AIMR (the Association for Investment Management and Research) had been formed in 1990.
