CFA Level I: format, topics and weights for 2026 and 2027

Level I tests breadth across ten topics in 180 multiple-choice questions. Here is the format, the topic weights, and what changes for exams from February 2027.

CFA Program guide · 2 min read

Level I is about breadth. It introduces the whole toolkit of investment analysis: ethics, statistics, economics, accounting, corporate finance, and the main asset classes. No single topic is very deep, but nothing can be skipped.

Format

  • 180 multiple-choice questions, each with three options.
  • Two sessions of 90 questions, about 2 hours 15 minutes each, with an optional break between them.
  • That gives you about 90 seconds per question, so pace matters as much as knowledge.
  • Exam windows: February, May, August and November.

Topic weights: 2026 against 2027

CFA Institute publishes each topic's weight as a range. For exams from February 2027 the ranges change: ethics shrinks, quantitative methods nearly doubles, and four topics are renamed.

Topic 2026 2027
Ethical and Professional Standards 15–20% 10–15%
Quantitative Methods 6–9% 11–14%
Economics 6–9% 6–9%
Financial Statement Analysis 11–14% 11–14%
Corporate Issuers (2027: Corporate Finance) 6–9% 6–9%
Equity Investments (2027: Equities) 11–14% 11–14%
Fixed Income 11–14% 11–14%
Derivatives (2027: Derivatives and Risk Management) 5–8% 6–9%
Alternative Investments 7–10% 6–9%
Portfolio Management (2027: Portfolio Construction) 8–12% 8–12%

What this means for how you study

  • Quantitative methods is now a heavyweight. From 2027 it carries as much weight as fixed income or equities. Treat time value of money, statistics and regression as core, not as a warm-up.
  • Ethics is smaller but still decisive. Ten to fifteen percent is around 18 to 27 questions. Candidates near the pass line are often separated by ethics, so keep doing practice questions until the end.
  • The big four (financial statements, equities, fixed income and quantitative methods) together make up roughly half of the exam.
  • Recent pass rate: 43% in August 2026, against a five-year average of 41%. Six in ten candidates do not pass, mostly because they under-practise rather than because the material is beyond them.
