Choosing CFA study materials without wasting money

By Gowtham KumarStudy blog

What the official curriculum gives you, what prep providers add, and how to choose: start from your weakness, not from a brand.

Every candidate has access to the official curriculum through CFA Institute. Prep providers sell condensed notes, video lectures, question banks and mock exams on top of it. The question is not which provider is "best", but which gap you need to fill.

What you already get from CFA Institute

  • The full curriculum with practice problems.
  • Mock exams included with registration, and an optional practice pack.
  • From 2026 at Level III, scoring rubrics for the written mocks.

The curriculum is thorough and long. Some candidates pass with it alone; most find it hard to finish in 300 hours.

What prep providers typically add

What you need What to look for
Less reading time condensed notes that follow the curriculum's structure
Understanding hard topics video lectures; try a free sample to see if the teaching style suits you
Practice volume large question banks with explanations and progress tracking
Exam rehearsal full-length mocks in the real format, especially Level II vignettes and Level III written answers
Accountability study planners, live classes or study groups

There are many providers, large and small. Most offer free trials or sample lessons, so test a provider's explanations and questions on a topic you find hard before you pay.

How to choose

  1. Start from the gap. If you understand well but run out of time, buy questions and mocks, not more video. If you get lost in the reading, buy teaching, not questions.
  2. Try before you buy. Watch one sample lesson on a topic you find hard. If it does not make it clearer in 20 minutes, it will not in 20 hours.
  3. Do not stack providers. Two sets of notes double the reading. One primary source plus one extra question bank is plenty.
  4. Check the curriculum year. Material must match your exam year's topic outline; 2027 changes Level I quantitative methods substantially.

Match the material to your situation

Your situation What usually helps most
Strong finance background, disciplined Official curriculum and question bank, plus one set of third-party mocks
New to finance Condensed notes or video lessons for the foundations, then the official question bank
Short on time Condensed notes, a question bank, and strict weekly targets
Failed before A fresh question bank and mocks from a different provider, plus an honest error log
Learn better by listening A video course, with questions after each session

What it costs

Question banks and mock packs usually cost a few hundred dollars per level; full video courses with notes roughly USD 500–1,500 per level. Everything you need to pass is in the curriculum included with your exam fee; extra materials buy speed and structure, not a pass.

Free help before you spend

Start with the free tools: the official curriculum and practice questions, our prerequisite concepts, the BA II Plus guide and the study planner. Buy more only when you know which gap you are filling.

Questions people ask

Is the official curriculum enough to pass?

Some candidates pass with it alone. Most add a question bank or condensed notes because the curriculum is long and time is short. Decide based on your weakest area, not on habit.

Which prep provider is best?

There is no single best. Choose by what you lack: teaching if the material is unclear, questions if you need practice, mocks if you need exam rehearsal. Try free samples before paying.

Should I buy materials from several providers?

Usually not. Two sets of notes double the reading. One main source plus one extra question bank or mock pack is plenty for most candidates.

Can I use last year's study materials?

Check the topic outline first. Some years change little; others, such as Level I in 2027, change a lot. Out-of-date material can cost more time than it saves.
