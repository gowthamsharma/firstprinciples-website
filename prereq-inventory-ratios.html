Inventory ratios: turnover and days on hand

By Gowtham KumarLesson 18 of 50

How quickly inventory sells says a lot about demand and management. Turnover and days on hand measure it, and together with receivables and payables days they give the cash conversion cycle.

Inventory turnover=Cost of salesAverage inventory,DOH=365Inventory turnover\text{Inventory turnover} = \frac{\text{Cost of sales}}{\text{Average inventory}},\qquad \text{DOH} = \frac{365}{\text{Inventory turnover}}Inventory turnover=Average inventoryCost of sales​,DOH=Inventory turnover365​

From first principles

Inventory is cash waiting on a shelf. The question for an analyst is how long it waits. Inventory turnover counts how many times the average stock is sold and replaced in a year; days of inventory on hand (DOH) turns that into days. Turnover uses cost of sales, not revenue, because inventory is carried at cost.

The same logic gives two more legs of the working capital cycle:

Receivables turnover=RevenueAverage receivablesDSO=365Receivables turnover\text{Receivables turnover} = \frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Average receivables}} \qquad \text{DSO} = \frac{365}{\text{Receivables turnover}}Receivables turnover=Average receivablesRevenue​DSO=Receivables turnover365​

Payables turnover=PurchasesAverage payablesDPO=365Payables turnover\text{Payables turnover} = \frac{\text{Purchases}}{\text{Average payables}} \qquad \text{DPO} = \frac{365}{\text{Payables turnover}}Payables turnover=Average payablesPurchases​DPO=Payables turnover365​

where purchases = cost of sales + closing inventory − opening inventory.

Put together:

Operating cycle=DOH+DSOCash conversion cycle=DOH+DSO−DPO\text{Operating cycle} = \text{DOH} + \text{DSO} \qquad \text{Cash conversion cycle} = \text{DOH} + \text{DSO} - \text{DPO}Operating cycle=DOH+DSOCash conversion cycle=DOH+DSO−DPO

The cash conversion cycle is how long the company's own cash is tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. Shorter is generally better, because less money is needed to run the business.

Worked example 1: turnover and days

Cost of sales 7,300; inventory 900 at the start of the year and 1,100 at the end, so the average is 1,000.

Turnover=7,3001,000=7.3DOH=3657.3=50 days\text{Turnover} = \frac{7{,}300}{1{,}000} = 7.3 \qquad \text{DOH} = \frac{365}{7.3} = 50 \text{ days}Turnover=1,0007,300​=7.3DOH=7.3365​=50 days

Worked example 2: the cash conversion cycle

The same company has revenue of 9,125 and average receivables of 1,000, so receivables turnover is 9.125 and DSO is 40 days. Purchases are 7,300+1,100−900=7,5007{,}300 + 1{,}100 - 900 = 7{,}5007,300+1,100−900=7,500 and average payables are 719, so payables turnover is about 10.4 and DPO about 35 days.

Cash conversion cycle=50+40−35=55 days\text{Cash conversion cycle} = 50 + 40 - 35 = 55 \text{ days}Cash conversion cycle=50+40−35=55 days

If the company can cut DOH to 40 days, its cycle falls to 45 days and roughly 10 days of cost of sales, about 200, is released as cash.

Reading the numbers

  • High turnover is not always good. It can mean too little stock and lost sales. Compare with revenue growth and with peers.
  • Falling turnover with slowing sales suggests obsolete inventory and a coming write-down.
  • Accounting choices move the ratios. With rising prices, LIFO gives higher cost of sales and lower inventory, so turnover looks much higher than for a FIFO or IFRS firm. Convert with the LIFO reserve before comparing.
  • Industry matters. A supermarket turns inventory over many times a year; an aircraft maker a few times.

Exam traps

  • Use cost of sales for inventory turnover and revenue for receivables turnover.
  • Use average balances where the question gives opening and closing figures.
  • Purchases, not cost of sales, drive payables: adjust for the change in inventory.
  • The cash conversion cycle subtracts DPO; the operating cycle does not.

Questions people ask

What is a good inventory turnover?

It depends on the industry. Perishable goods and fast-moving retail turn over many times a year; heavy machinery a few times. Compare a company with its own history and its closest peers.

What is the cash conversion cycle?

The number of days between paying suppliers and collecting cash from customers: days of inventory plus days of receivables minus days of payables. It shows how long the company's own cash is tied up in operations.

Why does LIFO affect inventory turnover?

With rising prices, LIFO charges newer, higher costs to cost of sales and leaves older, lower costs in inventory, so the ratio of the two is higher. The difference comes from the accounting method, not from selling faster.

Capitalise or expense?

Spending that benefits future years is capitalised as an asset and depreciated. The choice moves profit between years, moves cash between activities and changes ratios from return on assets to interest coverage.
