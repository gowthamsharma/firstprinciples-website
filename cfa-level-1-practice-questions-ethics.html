CFA Level I practice questions: Ethical and professional standards, with worked answers

By Gowtham KumarPractice questions

Ten original ethics questions in the Level I format, three options each, with an explanation for every answer. The Code of Ethics and the Standards of Professional Conduct, applied to cases. Ethical and professional standards carries 10–15% of the exam from 2027.

Ethics is the topic candidates most often leave too late. The questions are short cases: someone does something, and you decide which Standard applies and whether it was broken. The trick is to read for the duty involved (to clients, to employers, to the market) before you look at the answers.

Topic weight from 2027: 10–15% of Level I, about 18 to 27 of the 180 questions.

10 questions · Level I format · about 15 minutes

Choose an answer to see the explanation.

  1. An analyst overhears a company's CEO at a restaurant discussing an unannounced takeover. Under the Standards, the analyst should:

    Answer: A. Standard II(A), Material Nonpublic Information: how the information was obtained does not matter. If it is material and nonpublic, members and candidates must not act or cause others to act on it.

  2. A portfolio manager wants to buy a stock for her own account that she is also buying for clients. Under the Standards, she should:

    Answer: B. Standard VI(B), Priority of Transactions: clients' and employers' transactions come before personal ones. Owning the same securities is allowed, provided clients are not disadvantaged.

  3. An analyst changes her recommendation on a stock from buy to sell. To meet the Standards, she should:

    Answer: B. Standard III(B), Fair Dealing: clients must be treated fairly when recommendations are disseminated and actions taken. Firms may offer different service levels, but they must not disadvantage any client in getting investment recommendations.

  4. Which statement about the CFA Program is acceptable on a CV?

    Answer: A. Standard VII(B): the designation may be used only by charterholders, as an adjective ("Jane Smith, CFA"). Candidates may state facts about their progress, but there is no partial designation such as "CFA Level II", and "CFA" is never a noun.

  5. A client gives his portfolio manager a pair of theatre tickets to thank her for a strong year. Under the Standards, she should:

    Answer: C. Standard I(B), Independence and Objectivity: gifts from clients for past performance are less likely to compromise independence than gifts from issuers or brokers, but they should be disclosed to the employer, because they could create an incentive to favour that client.

  6. An employee plans to leave and start a competing firm. While still employed, he may:

    Answer: B. Standard IV(A), Loyalty: preparations that do not breach his duty to his employer are allowed. Soliciting clients or taking confidential records before leaving is a violation.

  7. A manager buys an illiquid, high-risk bond for a retired client whose policy statement calls for capital preservation. This most likely violates:

    Answer: A. Standard III(C), Suitability: investments must fit the client's written objectives and constraints and the portfolio as a whole. A speculative, illiquid bond conflicts with a capital-preservation mandate.

  8. An analyst copies several paragraphs from another firm's research into her own report without saying where they came from. This is:

    Answer: A. Standard I(C), Misrepresentation, includes plagiarism: presenting others' work or ideas as your own. She must cite the source or write her own analysis.

  9. To win new clients, a manager shows the returns of her three best-performing accounts as the firm's results. This:

    Answer: B. Standard III(D), Performance Presentation: results must be fair, accurate and complete. Cherry-picking the best accounts misleads prospects even if each number is correct. A composite of all similar accounts is the fair approach.

  10. A member works in a country whose laws on insider trading are less strict than the Code and Standards. She should:

    Answer: A. Standard I(A), Knowledge of the Law: members must follow the stricter of applicable law and the Code and Standards. Where local law is stricter, local law prevails.

Three things to remember for ethics

  • Name the Standard first, then decide. Most wrong answers describe a real rule that does not fit the facts.
  • When local law and the Code differ, the stricter rule wins.
  • Disclosure fixes many conflicts of interest, but not the use of material nonpublic information.

How to use these questions

Answer each question before you look at the explanation, and time yourself: the real exam allows about 90 seconds per question. Mark every question you got right for the wrong reason as wrong. When you miss one, write down the idea behind it rather than the answer, and come back to the page in a week.

These questions are written by First Principles to match the style and level of the exam. They are not taken from CFA Institute's curriculum or mock exams, which you should also use: they are included with your exam registration.

Questions people ask

Are these real CFA exam questions?

No. CFA Institute does not publish past exam questions. These are original questions written to match the Level I format: a short stem and three answer options, testing one idea each.

How many ethics questions are on Level I?

Ethical and professional standards carries 10–15% of the exam from 2027, which is roughly 18 to 27 of the 180 questions.

Where can I find more Level I practice questions?

Use the practice questions and mock exams in CFA Institute's learning ecosystem first; they come with registration. Then use one third-party question bank, and redo every question you missed.
