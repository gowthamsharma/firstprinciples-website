Are you ready for CFA Level I? A 5-minute self-check with a knowledge test

By Gowtham KumarStudy blog

Five questions about your time, English and goals, then ten real questions on statistics, compounding, accounting, economics and markets, each with a diagram and an explanation. You get a score by area and a plan for the gaps, before you pay the exam fee.

Registering for Level I is easy; being ready is not. Most people who fail Level I were not short of intelligence but of time, foundations or a plan. This check takes about five minutes. Part 1 asks about your situation; part 2 tests ten basics that Level I assumes you know, from the normal distribution to how a balance sheet balances, and explains each answer as you go.

What the check looks at, and why

Time (the biggest factor). People who pass typically report about 300 hours of study. That is 12 to 15 hours a week for five to six months. If you can only manage six hours a week, the same plan takes a year; register for a later window instead of rushing.

Maths. Level I does not need advanced maths, but it needs fluent algebra, compounding (1.05 to the power of 5), percentages and basic statistics. The calculator does the arithmetic; you must know what to ask it.

Accounting. Financial statement analysis is one of the largest Level I topics and the foundation of Level II. If you have never read a balance sheet, budget extra weeks.

Economics. A smaller topic, but supply and demand, inflation and exchange rates appear across the curriculum.

English. The exam is in English only, with long questions and tricky wording ("least likely", "except"). Practise timed reading early.

Purpose. A clear reason, such as a target job, carries you through month four, when motivation usually dips.

Eligibility. You need a bachelor's degree, to be in the final year of one, or 4,000 hours of work experience and/or higher education (over at least three years), plus a valid passport. See CFA requirements.

If you scored low on…

Gap What to do first Time to fix
Time Choose a later window; free up two evenings and one weekend morning a week Before registering
Maths Algebra and compounding refresher; learn the calculator's time value keys 2–3 weeks
Accounting The three statements, accruals and how they connect 3–4 weeks
Economics Demand and supply, GDP, inflation, exchange rates 1–2 weeks
English Timed practice with question banks from month one Ongoing

A five-question warm-up

Try these before you register. If three or more feel comfortable, your foundations are fine.

  1. What is 1,000 invested at 6% a year worth after 5 years? (About 1,338.)
  2. A company sells goods on credit in December but is paid in January. In which month is the revenue recorded? (December: accrual accounting records it when earned.)
  3. Demand for a product rises while supply stays the same. What happens to price? (It rises.)
  4. Returns of 4%, 10% and −2%: what is the arithmetic mean? (4%.)
  5. A bond's yield rises. Does its price rise or fall? (It falls.)

Questions people ask

Can a beginner pass CFA Level I?

Yes. Many candidates start with no finance background. Budget more than 300 hours, start with the foundations, and practise questions from the first month.

When should I start studying for CFA Level I?

Five to six months before your exam window at 12 to 15 hours a week, longer if you are new to accounting or can study fewer hours.

Do I need a finance degree for CFA Level I?

No. Any bachelor's degree, the final year of one, or 4,000 hours of work and/or higher education qualifies. Engineers, scientists and humanities graduates pass every year.

How do I know I'm ready to sit the exam?

When you score comfortably above about 70% in full, timed mock exams in the last weeks, and you have covered every topic at least once.
