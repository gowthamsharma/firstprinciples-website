CFA Level I practice questions: Economics, with worked answers

By Gowtham KumarPractice questions

Ten original economics questions in the Level I format, three options each, with an explanation for every answer. Demand and supply, market structures, the business cycle, money and exchange rates. Economics carries 6–9% of the exam from 2027.

Economics at Level I covers microeconomics (demand, costs, market structures), the business cycle, fiscal and monetary policy, trade and currencies. Calculations are short; most marks come from knowing which way something moves and why.

Topic weight from 2027: 6–9% of Level I, about 11 to 16 of the 180 questions.

10 questions · Level I format · about 15 minutes

Choose an answer to see the explanation.

  1. A café raises the price of coffee by 10% and sells 5% fewer cups. What happens to its revenue from coffee?

    Answer: B. Price elasticity = −5% / 10% = −0.5. In absolute terms that is below 1, so demand is inelastic: the price rise outweighs the lost volume and revenue rises (about 1.10 × 0.95 = 1.045, so roughly +4.5%).

  2. When the price of good X rises, demand for good Y rises. The two goods are most likely:

    Answer: B. A positive cross-price elasticity means buyers switch to Y when X becomes dearer: they are substitutes, like tea and coffee. Complements (cars and fuel) have a negative cross-price elasticity.

  3. In long-run equilibrium, a firm in a perfectly competitive market:

    Answer: B. Free entry and exit push price down to the lowest point of average total cost, where profit just covers the opportunity cost of capital (zero economic profit). Price equals marginal cost at that output.

  4. A profit-maximising monopolist chooses its output where:

    Answer: B. Like any firm, a monopolist maximises profit where MR = MC. Because it faces the downward-sloping market demand curve, the price it can charge at that output sits above MR and therefore above MC.

  5. Households spend 80% of each extra unit of disposable income, and the tax rate is 25%. What is the fiscal multiplier?

    Answer: C. Multiplier = 1 / [1 − MPC × (1 − t)] = 1 / [1 − 0.8 × 0.75] = 1 / 0.4 = 2.5. Ignoring taxes gives 1 / (1 − 0.8) = 5.0, which overstates the effect because part of each round of spending goes to the government.

  6. The money supply grows by 6% a year, velocity is constant and real output grows by 2% a year. According to the quantity theory of money, inflation will be about:

    Answer: B. MV = PY, so in growth rates: money growth + velocity growth = inflation + real growth. 6% + 0% = inflation + 2%, so inflation ≈ 4%.

  7. One euro buys 1.10 US dollars and one pound buys 1.30 US dollars. How many euros does one pound buy?

    Answer: B. Go through the dollar: 1 GBP = 1.30 USD, and 1.30 USD = 1.30 / 1.10 = 1.1818 EUR. 0.8462 is the number of pounds per euro; 1.43 multiplies the two quotes instead of dividing.

  8. The spot rate is 1.10 US dollars per euro. One-year interest rates are 5% in dollars and 3% in euros. What is the one-year forward rate, in dollars per euro?

    Answer: B. Covered interest parity: F = S × (1 + i_USD) / (1 + i_EUR) = 1.10 × 1.05 / 1.03 = 1.1214. The currency with the lower interest rate (the euro) trades at a forward premium, so the forward is above spot.

  9. Which of these is a leading indicator of the business cycle?

    Answer: B. Building permits turn before the economy does, because construction is planned months ahead. Industrial production moves with the cycle (coincident); the average length of unemployment changes after it (lagging).

  10. Country A can make one bolt of cloth by giving up 2 tonnes of wheat. Country B gives up 4 tonnes of wheat for one bolt. Which statement is correct?

    Answer: C. Comparative advantage depends on opportunity cost. Cloth costs A only 2 tonnes of wheat and B 4 tonnes, so A should make cloth and B wheat; both gain if they trade at a price between 2 and 4 tonnes of wheat per bolt.

Three things to remember for economics

  • For exchange rates, write the quote as price currency / base currency and check units before you divide.
  • Elasticity questions are really revenue questions: is the absolute value above or below 1?
  • Leading, coincident and lagging indicators are a list worth memorising.

How to use these questions

Answer each question before you look at the explanation, and time yourself: the real exam allows about 90 seconds per question. Mark every question you got right for the wrong reason as wrong. When you miss one, write down the idea behind it rather than the answer, and come back to the page in a week.

These questions are written by First Principles to match the style and level of the exam. They are not taken from CFA Institute's curriculum or mock exams, which you should also use: they are included with your exam registration.

Questions people ask

No. CFA Institute does not publish past exam questions. These are original questions written to match the Level I format: a short stem and three answer options, testing one idea each.

Economics carries 6–9% of the exam from 2027, which is roughly 11 to 16 of the 180 questions.

Use the practice questions and mock exams in CFA Institute's learning ecosystem first; they come with registration. Then use one third-party question bank, and redo every question you missed.
