The most challenging CFA topics at each level, and how to beat them

By Gowtham KumarStudy blog

Difficulty is weight times trouble. At Level I the danger is financial statements and fixed income; at Level II, the valuation-heavy vignettes in financial statements, equity and derivatives; at Level III, writing answers for asset allocation, derivatives and your pathway.

"Which topic is the hardest?" depends on you, but some topics trip up far more candidates than others. A useful way to rank them is weight × trouble: how many marks a topic carries, multiplied by how often candidates lose those marks. A hard topic worth 5% matters less than a tricky topic worth 15%.

Level I: breadth, accounting and bond maths

Topic Weight (2027) Why it hurts
Financial statement analysis 11–14% Huge volume, new vocabulary for non-accountants, and it is the base for Level II.
Fixed income 11–14% Duration, convexity and yield measures need calculation and intuition.
Quantitative methods 11–14% (up from 6–9%) Bigger in 2027; statistics and time value questions punish weak calculator skills.
Ethics 10–15% Feels like common sense but is not: the right answer follows the Standards, not instinct.
Derivatives 6–9% Abstract payoffs; easy to memorise and still misread.

How to beat them: learn financial statements from the logic of double entry (our free prerequisites start there); do bond price and duration questions by hand once, then on the calculator; practise ethics with hundreds of questions, not by reading. Use the BA II Plus guide until time value is automatic.

Level II: valuation under a case study

Level II is commonly described as the hardest exam by volume and depth. Every question sits in a vignette, so a weak topic costs a whole set of four questions.

Topic Weight Why it hurts
Financial statement analysis 10–15% Intercorporate investments, pensions, multinational operations, financial institutions: dense rules and adjustments.
Equity valuation 10–15% Many models (dividend discount, free cash flow, residual income, multiples) and many ways to misapply them.
Fixed income 10–15% Term structure, binomial trees, credit analysis and swaps.
Derivatives 5–10% Pricing and valuation of forwards, futures, swaps and options, with formulas that look alike.
Quantitative methods 5–10% Regression output tables you must interpret quickly.

How to beat them: know which model fits which situation, not just how to compute it. Practise reading the vignette first and the questions second, and do full item sets under time from the middle of your plan. See Level II item sets.

Level III: judgement, in writing

Half of Level III is constructed response: you write short answers and justify recommendations.

Topic Weight Why it hurts
Your pathway 30–35% The biggest block; depends on which pathway you choose.
Asset allocation 15–20% Applying frameworks to a client case, then defending the choice in words.
Portfolio construction 15–20% Equity, fixed income and alternative portfolio strategies; many small rules.
Derivatives and risk management 10–15% Hedging with options, futures and swaps in a portfolio context, often with calculations in written form.
Ethics 10–15% The same Code and Standards, applied to portfolio managers and their clients.

How to beat them: answer in short, exact sentences that use the command word (calculate, justify, recommend). Mark your own answers against official guideline answers. See Level III written answers.

The hidden challenges

Topics are not the only problem. Across all levels, candidates lose marks to:

  • Time pressure: about 90 seconds per question at Level I, 3 minutes at Level II.
  • Reading traps: "except", "least likely", "most appropriate".
  • Stale knowledge: a topic studied five months ago and never revisited.
  • Calculator slips: wrong mode (BGN), leftover values or too few decimals.

Fix these with timed question practice every week, a spaced-repetition review of old topics, and mock exams in the final six weeks.

Questions people ask

What is the hardest topic in CFA Level I?

For most candidates, financial statement analysis (large and new) and fixed income (calculation plus intuition). For non-finance candidates, accounting is usually the biggest hurdle.

What is the hardest topic in CFA Level II?

Financial statement analysis (pensions, intercorporate investments, multinationals) and equity valuation, because both are heavy and tested in vignettes where one misunderstanding costs several questions.

What is the hardest part of CFA Level III?

Writing constructed-response answers, especially for asset allocation, derivatives and your pathway, where you must justify recommendations clearly in limited time.

Which CFA level is the hardest?

Many candidates find Level II the hardest because of its depth and volume, but Level III's written format surprises people who were strong at multiple choice.
