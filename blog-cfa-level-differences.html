CFA Level I vs Level II vs Level III: how the exams differ

By Gowtham KumarStudy blog

Level I tests whether you know the tools, Level II whether you can value things with them, and Level III whether you can build and defend a portfolio for a real client.

The three CFA exams share a name and a curriculum style, but they test very different things. Knowing how they differ helps you change your study method at each step, which is where many candidates who passed one level stumble on the next.

Level I: the tools

  • Format: 180 standalone multiple-choice questions in two sessions of about 2¼ hours.
  • Content: ten broad topic areas, from ethics and quantitative methods to derivatives and portfolio management.
  • Skill: recall and apply. Most questions test one idea at a time.
  • Typical trap: breadth. A gap in any heavy topic is costly.
  • How to study: cover everything once, then practise many short questions.

Level II: valuation

  • Format: 22 case studies ("item sets"), each with four multiple-choice questions, about 4 hours 24 minutes in total.
  • Content: fewer topics but much deeper, with heavy emphasis on financial statements, equity valuation and fixed income.
  • Skill: apply and analyse. One case mixes several ideas and gives more data than you need.
  • Typical trap: reading speed and depth. Long vignettes punish slow reading and shallow understanding.
  • How to study: practise whole item sets under time, about 12 minutes each.

Level III: portfolio management

  • Format: 11 item sets plus 11 constructed-response (written-answer) sets, in two sessions.
  • Content: asset allocation, portfolio construction, performance measurement, derivatives and risk, ethics, plus your chosen pathway (about a third of the exam).
  • Skill: judge and recommend. You explain and justify decisions for a client.
  • Typical trap: writing too much or answering a different question. Command words matter.
  • How to study: write timed answers and compare them with official guideline answers.

What carries over

Ideas build on each other. Financial statement analysis and economics run through all three levels; time value of money and statistics never go away; ethics appears at every level. Understanding these foundations properly at Level I saves time at Levels II and III (see the prerequisite concepts).

The three exams at a glance

Level I Level II Level III
Question format 180 multiple choice 22 item sets (vignettes), 88 questions 11 item sets + 11 written (constructed-response) sets
Time 2 × 2 h 15 min 4 h 24 min 4 h 24 min
What it tests Knowing the tools Applying them to value assets Building and defending a portfolio for a client
Exam windows Feb, May, Aug, Nov May, Aug, Nov Feb, Aug
Recent pass rate about 43% about 45% about 47%
Hardest part for most Breadth and speed Depth and volume Writing under time pressure

How your study should change

  • Level I: cover everything once, then drill questions. Speed matters.
  • Level II: fewer topics, much deeper. Learn which model fits which situation. Practise full item sets, not single questions.
  • Level III: practise writing: short, exact answers to command words like justify and recommend, marked against official guideline answers.

How long each takes

Most people who pass report 300 hours or more per level; in a 2024 Kaplan Schweser survey, the share of recent passers studying 300+ hours was 50% at Level I, 73% at Level II and 62% at Level III. Plan more time for Level II than you did for Level I.

Questions people ask

Is Level II harder than Level I?

Most candidates say yes, because the material is deeper and every question sits inside a case. Pass rates are not very different, partly because the candidate group at Level II has already passed Level I.

Is Level III easier than Level II?

It is different rather than easier: less calculation, more judgement and writing. Candidates who are strong in calculation can find the written answers the toughest part.

Can I skip a level?

No. Each level must be passed before you can register for the next.
