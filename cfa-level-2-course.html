CFA Level II course: valuation, item sets and the reasoning behind the models

By Gowtham KumarThe course

The First Principles CFA Level II course: valuation models explained from the ground up, how to read a vignette before the questions, topic weights and hours, and the free material that prepares you now.

Level II asks you to apply the Level I tools, mostly to valuation, through 22 item sets of four questions each. One misunderstanding can cost a whole set, so the course concentrates on the reasoning behind each model: which one fits a case, and which assumption drives the answer.

What the course covers

Topic Share of exam What the lessons focus on
Financial statement analysis 10–15% Intercorporate investments, pensions, multinational operations, analyst adjustments
Equity valuation 10–15% Free cash flow, residual income, multiples, private company valuation
Fixed income 10–15% The term structure, arbitrage-free valuation, bonds with options, credit
Portfolio management 10–15% Multifactor models, risk measures, active management
Ethics 10–15% Cases on the Standards, in vignette form
Quantitative methods 5–10% Multiple regression and its pitfalls, time series, machine learning basics
Economics 5–10% Currency exchange rates, economic growth
Corporate issuers 5–10% Capital structure, dividends, ESG, mergers
Derivatives 5–10% Pricing and valuation of forwards, futures, swaps and options
Alternative investments 5–10% Real estate, private equity, commodities, hedge fund strategies

How the course handles item sets

Every topic ends with full item sets, worked the way the exam rewards: questions first, then the vignette, then each answer traced back to the line in the case that decides it. See how to pass Level II item sets.

Prepare now, free

Level II valuation leans on financial statement analysis and economics more than any other level. The prerequisites course covers deferred tax, the indirect cash flow method, inventories, exchange rates and more, one lesson at a time.

When it opens

Questions people ask

How is Level II different from Level I?

The topics overlap, but Level II goes deeper and tests application through case studies: you must decide which model fits and use it, not just recognise it. See how the levels differ.

How many hours does Level II need?

Most candidates who pass report more hours than for Level I: about 300 to 350, over five to six months.
