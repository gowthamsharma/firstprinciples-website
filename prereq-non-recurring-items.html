Non-recurring and non-operating items

By Gowtham KumarLesson 7 of 50

Analysts forecast what will happen again. The income statement's layers separate the repeatable core from one-offs and from businesses being sold.

Value comes from what will recur. Separate it from what will not.

From first principles

A valuation projects earnings into the future. If this year's profit includes a gain on selling a factory, projecting that gain forward would overstate value. So the income statement is built in layers that help separate:

  • Operating results: the business's core activity.
  • Non-operating items: interest income and expense, gains and losses on investments. Their classification can differ: for a bank, interest is operating.
  • Unusual or infrequent items: restructuring charges, impairments, litigation settlements. They are reported within continuing operations, before tax, often with disclosure.
  • Discontinued operations: a part of the business that has been sold or is held for sale and is clearly separable. Its results are shown net of tax, below profit from continuing operations.

Worked example

Operating profit 200, interest expense 20, a one-off restructuring charge of 30, tax at 25%, and a division being sold that lost 12 after tax. Reported net income =(200−20−30)×0.75−12= (200 - 20 - 30) \times 0.75 - 12=(200−20−30)×0.75−12 = 100.5. A sustainable estimate excludes the restructuring and the discontinued division: (200−20)×0.75(200 - 20) \times 0.75(200−20)×0.75 = 135.

Exam traps

  • US GAAP no longer has "extraordinary items", and IFRS never allows them.
  • "Non-recurring" items that recur every year (restructuring every year) are really operating costs.
  • Changes in accounting policy are applied retrospectively (restating prior years); changes in estimates go forward only; errors are corrected by restating.

Questions people ask

Why separate one-off items from recurring profit?

Valuation is about the future. A gain from selling a building will not happen again, so an analyst removes it to see the earnings power the business can keep producing.

When is a business a discontinued operation?

When a whole component that can be clearly distinguished, such as a major line of business or region, has been sold or is held for sale. Its results are shown separately, net of tax, below continuing operations.

Are restructuring charges really one-off?

Not always. Some companies report them almost every year. If an item recurs, treat it as a normal cost of doing business, whatever the label says.
