Inflation and price indexes

By Gowtham KumarLesson 43 of 50

Inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level. How it is measured, and how deflation, disinflation and hyperinflation differ.

Inflation rate=IndextIndext−1−1\text{Inflation rate} = \frac{\text{Index}_t}{\text{Index}_{t-1}} - 1Inflation rate=Indext−1​Indext​​−1

From first principles

Prices change for thousands of goods at once. To summarise them, statisticians price a basket of goods over time and express its cost as an index (base period = 100). Which basket?

  • Laspeyres index: the base-period basket. Most consumer price indexes work this way.
  • Paasche index: the current-period basket.
  • Fisher index: the geometric mean of the two.

Laspeyres=∑PtQ0∑P0Q0×100Paasche=∑PtQt∑P0Qt×100Fisher=L×P\text{Laspeyres} = \frac{\sum P_t Q_0}{\sum P_0 Q_0} \times 100 \qquad \text{Paasche} = \frac{\sum P_t Q_t}{\sum P_0 Q_t} \times 100 \qquad \text{Fisher} = \sqrt{L \times P}Laspeyres=∑P0​Q0​∑Pt​Q0​​×100Paasche=∑P0​Qt​∑Pt​Qt​​×100Fisher=L×P​

Why a fixed basket overstates inflation

  • Substitution bias: people buy less of what got dearer. A Laspeyres index keeps the old quantities, so it overstates the rise in the cost of living; Paasche tends to understate it.
  • Quality bias: a better phone at the same price is effectively a price cut. Hedonic adjustments try to correct for quality.
  • New-product bias: new goods enter the basket late, after their prices have fallen most.

Headline and core

Headline inflation includes everything; core inflation excludes volatile food and energy, which makes the underlying trend easier to see. Central banks watch core closely because a one-off energy shock does not need a policy response unless it feeds into wages and expectations.

Words that matter

  • Disinflation: inflation falls but stays positive (from 6% to 3%). Prices still rise.
  • Deflation: prices fall (inflation below 0). Dangerous because it raises the real burden of debt and encourages delaying purchases.
  • Hyperinflation: extremely high, accelerating inflation, usually from printing money to fund deficits.
  • Cost-push inflation comes from supply (wages, commodities); demand-pull inflation from spending above potential.

Exam traps

  • Disinflation still means rising prices.
  • A Laspeyres index overstates the cost of living; Paasche understates it; Fisher sits between.
  • Inflation is the change in the index, not the index level.
  • Core inflation excludes food and energy, not "all volatile items".

Questions people ask

How is a consumer price index built?

Statisticians price a fixed basket of goods and services over time and compare its cost with a base period. The basket is weighted by how households actually spend.

What is the difference between headline and core inflation?

Headline inflation covers everything; core removes volatile food and energy prices. Central banks watch core because it shows the persistent trend better.

Why can a price index overstate inflation?

A fixed basket misses that people switch to cheaper goods, that quality improves and that new products appear. These biases push measured inflation slightly above the true cost of living.
