CFA Level I practice questions: Equity investments, with worked answers

By Gowtham KumarPractice questions

Ten original equity investments questions in the Level I format, three options each, with an explanation for every answer. Market mechanics, indexes, efficiency, industry analysis and valuation models. Equity investments carries 11–14% of the exam from 2027.

Equity investments covers how markets work (orders, margin, indexes), market efficiency, industry and company analysis, and valuation with dividend discount models and multiples. Expect several margin and index calculations and at least one Gordon growth model.

Topic weight from 2027: 11–14% of Level I, about 20 to 25 of the 180 questions.

10 questions · Level I format · about 15 minutes

Choose an answer to see the explanation.

  1. A stock just paid a dividend of 2.00. Dividends are expected to grow by 5% a year forever, and investors require a 9% return. What is the stock worth today?

    Answer: C. Gordon growth model: V₀ = D₁ / (r − g) = 2.00 × 1.05 / (0.09 − 0.05) = 2.10 / 0.04 = 52.50. 50.00 uses this year's dividend instead of next year's.

  2. A company pays out 40% of earnings. Its required return is 10% and dividends grow at 6%. What is its justified forward P/E?

    Answer: C. Justified forward P/E = payout ratio / (r − g) = 0.40 / (0.10 − 0.06) = 10.0.

  3. If a market is semi-strong form efficient, which of these will NOT consistently earn abnormal returns?

    Answer: B. Semi-strong efficiency means prices already reflect all public information, so fundamental analysis of published data cannot beat the market after costs. Inside information is not public. An index fund does not try to earn abnormal returns.

  4. You buy a stock at 50 with an initial margin of 50%. The maintenance margin is 25%. Below what price will you receive a margin call?

    Answer: C. You borrowed 25 per share. A call comes when equity / price falls to 25%: (P − 25) / P = 0.25, so P = 25 / 0.75 = 33.33. The general formula is P₀ × (1 − initial margin) / (1 − maintenance margin).

  5. A price-weighted index holds three stocks priced 10, 20 and 70. Only the 70 stock rises, by 10%. What is the index return?

    Answer: B. A price-weighted index moves with the sum of prices: (10 + 20 + 77) / (10 + 20 + 70) − 1 = 7.0%. Expensive stocks dominate. An equal-weighted index would rise 10% / 3 = 3.3%.

  6. A preferred share pays a fixed dividend of 5 a year with no maturity. Investors require 8%. What is it worth?

    Answer: B. A perpetual fixed dividend is a perpetuity: V = D / r = 5 / 0.08 = 62.50.

  7. In which stage of the industry life cycle do growth slow, competition become intense and weaker firms leave?

    Answer: B. In the shakeout, demand still grows but more slowly, capacity overshoots and price competition forces consolidation. In maturity growth is low but competition is more stable.

  8. According to Porter's five forces, an industry is most likely to be highly profitable when:

    Answer: B. High barriers keep new competitors out and weak buyers cannot push prices down, so firms keep more of the value they create. Substitutes and powerful suppliers both squeeze profits.

  9. You buy 100 shares at 40 using 50% margin and sell them at 46. Ignoring interest, fees and dividends, what return do you earn on your own money?

    Answer: C. You put in 2,000 of your own money (50% of 4,000). The gain is 100 × 6 = 600, so the return on equity is 600 / 2,000 = 30%: twice the 15% move in the price, because of leverage.

  10. A company's enterprise value is valued at 12 times EBITDA of 500. Net debt is 1,000 and there are 100 shares. What is the value per share?

    Answer: B. EV = 12 × 500 = 6,000. Equity = EV − net debt = 5,000, so 50.00 a share. 60.00 forgets to subtract the debt; 70.00 adds it instead.

Three things to remember for equity investments

  • The Gordon growth model needs next year's dividend, D1, not the one just paid.
  • Price-weighted indexes are moved by high-priced stocks; value-weighted by large companies.
  • Subtract net debt to get from enterprise value to equity value.

How to use these questions

Answer each question before you look at the explanation, and time yourself: the real exam allows about 90 seconds per question. Mark every question you got right for the wrong reason as wrong. When you miss one, write down the idea behind it rather than the answer, and come back to the page in a week.

These questions are written by First Principles to match the style and level of the exam. They are not taken from CFA Institute's curriculum or mock exams, which you should also use: they are included with your exam registration.

Questions people ask

Are these real CFA exam questions?

No. CFA Institute does not publish past exam questions. These are original questions written to match the Level I format: a short stem and three answer options, testing one idea each.

How many equity investments questions are on Level I?

Equity investments carries 11–14% of the exam from 2027, which is roughly 20 to 25 of the 180 questions.

Where can I find more Level I practice questions?

Use the practice questions and mock exams in CFA Institute's learning ecosystem first; they come with registration. Then use one third-party question bank, and redo every question you missed.
