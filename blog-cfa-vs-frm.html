CFA vs FRM: which should you do?

The CFA is the broad investment credential; the FRM is the specialist risk credential. Choose by the job you want, and know that many risk professionals end up holding both.

2 min read  /  By Gowtham Kumar

The CFA (from CFA Institute) and the FRM (from GARP, the Global Association of Risk Professionals) are often compared because both are rigorous, global and finance-focused. They are built for different jobs.

At a glance

CFA FRM
Awarded by CFA Institute GARP
Exams 3 levels 2 parts
Study time about 300 hours per level about 240 hours per part
Exam fees USD 3,520–4,570 for all three USD 1,600–2,000 for both parts, plus a one-time USD 400 enrolment fee
Work experience 4,000 hours over at least 36 months 2 years of relevant full-time work
Focus investment analysis and portfolio management market, credit, operational and liquidity risk
Typical roles research, portfolio management, wealth risk management, risk modelling, treasury, regulation

How the exams differ

  • FRM Part I (100 multiple-choice questions, four hours) covers foundations, quantitative analysis, markets and products, and valuation and risk models.
  • FRM Part II (80 questions, four hours) goes deep into market, credit, operational and liquidity risk, plus investment risk and current issues.
  • FRM exams are offered in May, August and November, and Part II must be passed within four years of Part I.

The CFA covers far more ground (accounting, equity, fixed income, economics, ethics, portfolio management), while the FRM goes deeper and more quantitatively into risk.

Which is harder?

The CFA is the bigger commitment: three exams, around 900 hours and usually several years. The FRM is shorter but more quantitative, especially Part II. Neither is easy.

Which to choose

  • Choose the CFA for investment research, portfolio or fund management, wealth management, or if you are not yet sure of your path. It is the broader door-opener.
  • Choose the FRM if you are in, or aiming for, risk management at a bank, asset manager, insurer or regulator.
  • Do both if you work in investment risk: the overlap in quantitative methods, fixed income and derivatives makes the second credential faster.

Questions people ask

Should I do CFA or FRM first?

Start with the one that matches your current or next job. Many people take FRM Part I while waiting between CFA levels, because the overlapping material is fresh.

Is the FRM easier than the CFA?

It is shorter, with two exams instead of three, but its risk content is more quantitative. Most candidates find the total effort of the CFA larger.

Is FRM recognised outside banks?

Yes, at asset managers, insurers, consultancies and regulators, wherever risk is measured and managed.
