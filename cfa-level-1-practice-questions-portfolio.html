CFA Level I practice questions: Portfolio management, with worked answers

By Gowtham KumarPractice questions

Ten original portfolio management questions in the Level I format, three options each, with an explanation for every answer. Risk and return, diversification, the CAPM, performance measures and the IPS. Portfolio management carries 8–12% of the exam from 2027.

Portfolio management ties the curriculum together: risk and return, diversification, the CAPM and security market line, performance measures, the investment policy statement and risk management. Several questions need the two-asset portfolio variance formula.

Topic weight from 2027: 8–12% of Level I, about 14 to 22 of the 180 questions.

10 questions · Level I format · about 15 minutes

Choose an answer to see the explanation.

  1. A portfolio is 60% in asset A (standard deviation 20%) and 40% in asset B (standard deviation 10%). Their correlation is 0.2. What is the portfolio's standard deviation?

    Answer: A. σ² = 0.6² × 0.20² + 0.4² × 0.10² + 2 × 0.6 × 0.4 × 0.2 × 0.20 × 0.10 = 0.0144 + 0.0016 + 0.00192 = 0.01792; σ = 13.39%. 16% is the weighted average, which would apply only with a correlation of 1: diversification lowers risk.

  2. The risk-free rate is 3% and the market's expected return is 9%. What is the CAPM expected return for a stock with a beta of 0.8?

    Answer: B. E(R) = 3% + 0.8 × (9% − 3%) = 3% + 4.8% = 7.8%. 7.2% multiplies the whole market return by beta.

  3. A portfolio returned 10% with a standard deviation of 16%. The risk-free rate was 2%. What is its Sharpe ratio?

    Answer: C. Sharpe ratio = (10% − 2%) / 16% = 0.50: excess return per unit of total risk.

  4. Which risk can be reduced by holding many different securities?

    Answer: A. Company-specific surprises tend to cancel out across a large portfolio. Market-wide risk affects all securities together and cannot be diversified away, which is why only it earns a premium in the CAPM.

  5. A client is very willing to take risk, but her ability to take risk is low because she needs the money for a house purchase in two years. In the investment policy statement, her risk tolerance should be:

    Answer: A. When ability and willingness conflict, the adviser usually sets risk tolerance by the lower of the two and explains why. A short horizon and a fixed liability limit how much loss she can absorb.

  6. A portfolio holds 50% in a stock with a beta of 1.4 and 50% in a stock with a beta of 0.6. What is the portfolio's beta?

    Answer: A. Portfolio beta is the weighted average of the betas: 0.5 × 1.4 + 0.5 × 0.6 = 1.0. Unlike standard deviation, beta needs no correlation term.

  7. A fund returned 12%. Given its beta, the CAPM predicted 10%. What is its Jensen's alpha?

    Answer: A. Jensen's alpha = actual return − CAPM-required return = 12% − 10% = +2%: the fund beat what its market risk justified.

  8. An investor with a risk-aversion coefficient of 4 considers a portfolio with an expected return of 10% and a standard deviation of 20%. Using U = E(R) − ½Aσ², what is its utility?

    Answer: B. U = 0.10 − 0.5 × 4 × 0.20² = 0.10 − 0.08 = 0.02, or 2%. Utility falls quickly as risk rises for a risk-averse investor.

  9. A portfolio returned 11% with a beta of 0.8. The risk-free rate was 3%. What is its Treynor ratio?

    Answer: A. Treynor ratio = (11% − 3%) / 0.8 = 10%: excess return per unit of systematic risk. It suits well-diversified portfolios, where beta captures most of the risk.

  10. A portfolio has a one-day 5% value at risk of 1 million. This means:

    Answer: A. VaR is a threshold: losses at least this large happen with the stated probability. It says nothing about how large losses are beyond the threshold, which is why it is paired with stress tests and expected shortfall.

Three things to remember for portfolio management

  • Only correlations below 1 reduce portfolio risk.
  • Sharpe uses total risk; Treynor and Jensen's alpha use beta.
  • When ability and willingness to take risk differ, the lower one usually sets the policy.

How to use these questions

Answer each question before you look at the explanation, and time yourself: the real exam allows about 90 seconds per question. Mark every question you got right for the wrong reason as wrong. When you miss one, write down the idea behind it rather than the answer, and come back to the page in a week.

These questions are written by First Principles to match the style and level of the exam. They are not taken from CFA Institute's curriculum or mock exams, which you should also use: they are included with your exam registration.

Questions people ask

Are these real CFA exam questions?

No. CFA Institute does not publish past exam questions. These are original questions written to match the Level I format: a short stem and three answer options, testing one idea each.

How many portfolio management questions are on Level I?

Portfolio management carries 8–12% of the exam from 2027, which is roughly 14 to 22 of the 180 questions.

Use the practice questions and mock exams in CFA Institute's learning ecosystem first; they come with registration. Then use one third-party question bank, and redo every question you missed.
