What financial reporting is for

Financial statements exist to help investors and lenders decide whether to provide capital. Everything else in accounting follows from that purpose.

Concept 1 of 50 · Financial Statement Analysis · Introduction to financial reporting · 2 min read

Accounts are a decision tool for people who give a company money. Judge every rule by whether it helps them decide.

From first principles

Investors and lenders cannot see inside a company. They need a standard report that tells them what the company owns and owes, how it performed, and how its cash moved. The objective of general-purpose financial reporting under the IFRS framework is exactly that: to provide information that is useful to existing and potential investors and lenders in deciding whether to provide resources to the entity.

Two questions follow:

  • Would this information change a decision? If not, it is clutter. That is relevance, which includes materiality: an error is material if it could change a user's decision.
  • Does it describe what really happened? That is faithful representation: complete, neutral (not tilted to look good) and free from error.

Four enhancing qualities make relevant and faithful information more useful: comparability (across firms and years), verifiability, timeliness and understandability. The constraint is cost: information has to be worth more than it costs to produce. There is also a natural tension: the most timely information is often less verifiable.

Worked example

A retailer could report each store's monthly revenue. Is it material? For a firm with 3 stores, yes: one store failing changes the outlook. For a firm with 3,000 stores, one store's revenue is immaterial, but the pattern across regions is not. Same information, different relevance.

Exam traps

  • Relevance and faithful representation are the two fundamental qualities. The other four are enhancing, not fundamental.
  • Neutrality is not conservatism. Deliberately understating assets is a bias, just as overstating them is.
  • The going-concern assumption, that the business will carry on, underlies the statements. If it fails, a different basis of preparation is needed.

Why it matters at Level III

Every analyst adjustment you make later, from restating inventory to removing goodwill, is an attempt to restore comparability or a faithful picture. Knowing the purpose tells you which adjustments are worth making.
