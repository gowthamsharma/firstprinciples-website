The method

What is a first-principles approach?

A first principle is a statement that cannot be reduced any further: it is true by definition or by logic, not because a textbook says so. Reasoning from first principles means taking a problem apart until only those statements are left, then rebuilding the answer from them, one step at a time.

The CFA curriculum runs to thousands of pages, yet almost all of it rests on a handful of such statements. Learn them properly and most formulas stop being things to remember. They become things you can work out, check and explain, which is exactly what Level II and Level III ask you to do.

The ideas almost everything rests on

  • Money now is worth more than money laterDiscounting, bond pricing, valuation, capital budgeting
  • Assets = liabilities + equity, alwaysEvery accounting entry, the three statements, analyst adjustments
  • Identical cash flows have one priceNo-arbitrage pricing of forwards, swaps and options
  • Investors want more return for more riskRequired returns, the cost of capital, the CAPM
  • Risk that can be diversified away earns nothingPortfolio theory, beta, asset allocation
  • Prices move until supply meets demandElasticity, market structures, exchange rates, interest rates
1

Take it apart

Ask what has to be true for the result to hold, and keep asking until you reach a statement like the ones above.

2

Rebuild it, one step at a time

Add one idea at a time, with a picture at every step, and check that each step follows from the one before.

3

Name the formula last, then test it

The formula only writes down what you can already see. Then predict the answer before you calculate it, and find out why if you were wrong.

An example: why bond prices fall when rates rise

Memorise: "price and yield are inversely related."

Reason: a bond is a fixed set of future payments. Its price today is what those payments are worth today. A higher interest rate means each future payment is worth less today, so the total, the price, must fall. And the further away a payment is, the more it shrinks. That last sentence is all duration really is.
