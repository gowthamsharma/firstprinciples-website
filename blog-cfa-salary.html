CFA salary by country: what charterholders earn around the world

By Gowtham KumarStudy blog

CFA pay in 19 countries, from the United States, Switzerland and Singapore to Germany, the UAE, India and Nigeria, in local currency and US dollars. What drives the gaps, why different sources disagree, and how to read any CFA salary figure.

"How much does a CFA earn?" is one of the most searched questions about the program in every country where people take it. The honest answer starts with a warning: there is no single CFA salary. Pay in investment work depends on the job, the city, the size of the firm and, above all, years of experience. The charter changes which jobs you can get and how quickly you are trusted with more. The market sets the price of those jobs.

With that in mind, comparing one consistent source across countries shows clearly where the money is and how far apart the markets are.

CFA salaries in 19 countries

The table uses one source for every country: the average base salary reported to PayScale by people who list the CFA among their qualifications. That group mixes charterholders with candidates who have passed one or two levels, and new graduates with senior managers, so read the numbers as typical, not as what a charterholder with ten years' experience earns. Small samples are flagged; treat those as rough.

Add the bonus

These are base salaries. In investment roles a bonus usually comes on top: in PayScale's US data the typical bonus is about 5–10% of base for analysts, 10–15% for portfolio and risk managers and over 20% for private bankers, and the top 10% of bonuses reach $35,000 for portfolio managers and $120,000 for chief investment officers. In Singapore and Hong Kong, bonuses are a larger share still: a typical portfolio manager's bonus there is a quarter to a third of base pay. See CFA jobs and salaries for base plus bonus by role and country.

Why the numbers differ so much

Four forces explain almost all of the gap between the top and the bottom of the table.

  1. The size of the local investment industry. Switzerland, the United States, Hong Kong, Singapore and London hold enormous pools of managed money. Where there is more money to manage, the people who manage and analyse it are paid more.
  2. General wage levels. A finance salary is high or low compared with other salaries in the same country. In dollars, an Indian or Pakistani figure looks small; against local graduate pay, it is high. Compare with local wages and the cost of living, not across borders.
  3. Base versus total pay. The table shows base salary only. In asset management, research and wealth management a bonus often adds a meaningful share on top, and in senior roles it can exceed the base. In the Gulf, housing and schooling allowances, plus the absence of income tax, make the real package much larger than the base suggests.
  4. Who answers. Self-reported figures mix candidates with charterholders and juniors with seniors. A country where many respondents are early-career Level I candidates will show a lower average than one dominated by experienced fund managers.

Region by region

North America

The United States has the deepest market for charterholders and the largest sample: an average base around $110,000, with most respondents between roughly $65,000 and $190,000 depending on experience. Portfolio management, buy-side research and hedge funds pay most; regional banks and corporate finance roles less. Canada is lower in dollar terms (about C$89,000 base), but Toronto's pension funds and asset managers are among the most charter-heavy employers anywhere.

Europe

Switzerland tops the table, reflecting Zurich and Geneva private banks and asset managers and the country's high general wages. Germany (Frankfurt, Munich), the Netherlands (pension fund managers) and Luxembourg (the fund-administration hub) sit close together around €70,000–76,000 base. The United Kingdom average looks modest at £61,000 because it includes many junior roles across the country; London buy-side and research roles pay well above it, and bonuses are common. France and Ireland have small samples, so treat their figures as indicative.

Middle East

In the UAE the base salary (about AED 175,000) understates the package. Many employers add housing, transport and schooling allowances, and there is no personal income tax, so take-home pay compares well with Europe. Sovereign wealth funds and family offices in Abu Dhabi and Dubai are among the employers that value the charter most.

Asia-Pacific

Hong Kong and Singapore are Asia's two big asset-management centres and pay accordingly; bonuses are a large part of total pay there. Australia's superannuation industry makes it a strong market too. China's figure comes from a very small sample and Shanghai or Beijing roles at large firms pay far more. In India, Pakistan and the Philippines, dollar figures are low but local purchasing power is high, and the charter is a strong signal because it is still relatively rare.

Africa

South Africa has Africa's largest investment industry (Johannesburg and Cape Town asset managers), with an average base near R500,000. Nigeria's sample is small and from 2023; Lagos asset managers, pension fund administrators and banks are the main employers of charterholders there.

India in detail: why two sources disagree by a factor of three

India shows how much the source matters. PayScale's average for people listing the CFA is about ₹10 lakh a year. CFA Institute's own 2026 study for India, run by Kantar with about 2,770 respondents, found:

  • charterholders awarded in 2024–2025, with about six years' experience on average, reported income of about ₹32 lakh a year;
  • all charterholders, averaging about ten years' experience, reported about ₹67 lakh;
  • more than 70% of charterholders with eight or more years' experience earned over ₹40 lakh.

The same study shows how fast Indian pay is moving: average pay across all respondents, including candidates and aspirants, rose from ₹19.3 lakh in 2022 to ₹22.1 lakh in 2026, and 69% received raises of more than 20% in 2025. Dollar comparisons made a few years ago now understate Indian investment salaries.

Both sources are probably right. The first mixes candidates and early-career analysts; the second counts only charterholders, most of them experienced. The same gap would appear in any country if you could compare like with like: experience and the charter together are what move pay, not exams passed.

What changes pay more than the country

  • Role. Portfolio managers and buy-side analysts are paid more than operations, performance or client-reporting roles, even at the same firm.
  • Experience. Pay typically rises steeply in the first ten years.
  • Firm type. Hedge funds and large asset managers pay more than banks' back offices or small brokers.
  • Charter versus candidate. Passing a level rarely brings a raise on its own; the full charter, plus the experience it requires, opens senior roles.
  • Extra skills. Programming (Python, SQL), data skills or a sector specialism on top of the charter are rare and well paid in every market.

How to read any CFA salary figure

  1. Ask what kind of pay it is: base salary, base plus bonus, or total package with allowances.
  2. Ask who was counted: charterholders only, or anyone with CFA on their profile; one city or the whole country.
  3. Check the year and the sample size. A figure from 15 people in 2023 is a hint, not a fact.
  4. Compare in local currency against local wages, not in dollars across countries.
  5. Look for a local CFA society survey. Many societies survey their members; even a summary is more reliable than a job-site average.

Questions people ask

Which country pays CFA charterholders the most?

In this comparison, Switzerland and the United States have the highest average base salaries, followed by Hong Kong and Singapore. Counting tax and allowances, the UAE moves up sharply. In every country, senior portfolio managers earn far more than the averages shown.

What is the CFA salary in Dubai and the UAE?

The reported average base is about AED 175,000 a year (about $48,000). Packages often add housing and other allowances, and there is no personal income tax, so total take-home pay is much higher than the base.

What is the CFA salary in Germany?

About €76,000 base on average among people who list the CFA, mostly in Frankfurt and Munich. Asset managers, banks and index providers are the main employers; bonuses come on top.

What is the salary after CFA Level 3?

It depends on your role and experience more than on the exam. Passing Level III and receiving the charter (which also needs 4,000 hours of relevant experience) opens senior investment roles; the pay comes from those roles.

Is the CFA worth it for the salary alone?

Rarely. The charter pays off when you work in, or want to move into, investment roles where it is valued: research, portfolio management, wealth management and risk. See is the CFA worth it for the full case.

How much does the CFA cost in total?

Exam fees alone come to USD 3,520 to 4,570 for all three levels. Add study materials and any retakes, and most candidates spend somewhere between USD 4,000 and 8,000.

Study strategyBy Gowtham KumarStudy blog3 min read

How long does the CFA take?

The fastest possible route through all three exams is about a year, and 18 months is a more realistic fast track; most candidates take two to four years. Then you need 4,000 hours of work experience for the charter.

Study strategyBy Gowtham KumarStudy blog4 min read

CFA exam results: when they arrive and how to read them

Results come by email five to nine weeks after your exam window closes, as a simple pass or fail plus a score report. The pass mark is never published, so the report is about where you stand, not a number to hit.
