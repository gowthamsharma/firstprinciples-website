CFA Level I practice questions: Financial statement analysis, with worked answers

By Gowtham KumarPractice questions

Ten original financial statement analysis questions in the Level I format, three options each, with an explanation for every answer. The three statements, inventories, long-lived assets, taxes, debt and ratio analysis. Financial statement analysis carries 11–14% of the exam from 2027.

Financial statement analysis is one of the four heavyweights at Level I. Questions test whether you can follow a transaction through the three statements, compare IFRS with US GAAP, and compute ratios without mixing up averages and year-end figures.

Topic weight from 2027: 11–14% of Level I, about 20 to 25 of the 180 questions.

10 questions · Level I format · about 15 minutes

Choose an answer to see the explanation.

  1. Prices are rising. Compared with FIFO, a company using LIFO will most likely report:

    Answer: A. LIFO charges the most recent, more expensive purchases to COGS and leaves older, cheaper units in inventory. Profits and taxes are lower; the balance sheet understates inventory. (LIFO is allowed under US GAAP, not under IFRS.)

  2. Equipment costs 10,000, lasts five years and has no salvage value. What is the first-year depreciation under the double-declining-balance method?

    Answer: B. The straight-line rate is 1/5 = 20%, so the double-declining rate is 40%, applied to the full opening book value: 40% × 10,000 = 4,000. 2,000 is straight-line; 3,200 is the second year's DDB charge (40% × 6,000).

  3. A company has cash of 50, receivables of 100, inventory of 150 and current liabilities of 200. What are its current ratio and quick ratio?

    Answer: B. Current ratio = (50 + 100 + 150) / 200 = 1.50. The quick ratio leaves out inventory, the least liquid current asset: (50 + 100) / 200 = 0.75.

  4. Net income is 100, depreciation 20, receivables rose by 15 and payables rose by 10. What is cash flow from operations (indirect method)?

    Answer: B. Start from net income and undo non-cash items and working-capital changes: 100 + 20 (depreciation) − 15 (cash not yet collected) + 10 (bills not yet paid) = 115.

  5. Net profit margin is 5%, asset turnover is 2.0 and assets are 1.5 times equity. What is the return on equity?

    Answer: B. DuPont: ROE = margin × turnover × leverage = 5% × 2.0 × 1.5 = 15%. The decomposition shows where returns come from: profitability, efficiency and borrowing.

  6. A deferred tax liability most likely arises when:

    Answer: C. Faster tax depreciation means less tax is paid now than the accounts imply; the difference will reverse later, so the company owes tax in the future. The other two situations create deferred tax assets.

  7. Compared with expensing it, capitalising a cost in the year it is paid will:

    Answer: B. A capitalised cost goes on the balance sheet and is depreciated, so year-one profit is higher. The cash still leaves, but it is classified as investing rather than operating, so CFO rises and CFI falls. Total cash flow is unchanged.

  8. Where can a company report interest paid in its statement of cash flows?

    Answer: A. IFRS lets a company choose operating or financing activities for interest paid (and be consistent). US GAAP requires operating. This is a frequent source of differences when you compare cash flows across companies.

  9. Under IFRS, a company wrote its inventory down to net realisable value last year. This year the value recovers. What can it do?

    Answer: C. IFRS allows reversal of an inventory write-down, limited to the amount originally written down. US GAAP does not allow reversals for most inventory. Neither allows writing inventory above cost.

  10. A bond is issued at a discount. Under the effective interest method, its interest expense each year is:

    Answer: B. Interest expense = market rate at issue × carrying amount. A discount bond's market rate exceeds its coupon rate, so expense exceeds the cash coupon; the difference is added to the carrying amount, which climbs to face value by maturity.

Three things to remember for financial statement analysis

  • Ask what each choice does to the income statement, the balance sheet and cash flow, in that order.
  • Under rising prices, LIFO lowers profit, inventory and taxes; FIFO does the reverse.
  • Memorise the IFRS and US GAAP differences in cash flow classification.

How to use these questions

Answer each question before you look at the explanation, and time yourself: the real exam allows about 90 seconds per question. Mark every question you got right for the wrong reason as wrong. When you miss one, write down the idea behind it rather than the answer, and come back to the page in a week.

These questions are written by First Principles to match the style and level of the exam. They are not taken from CFA Institute's curriculum or mock exams, which you should also use: they are included with your exam registration.
