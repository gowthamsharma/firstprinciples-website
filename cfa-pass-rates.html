CFA pass rates, session by session

By Gowtham KumarFacts checked 16 November 2026

The latest pass rates for every level, the long-run averages, and the one statistic that matters most for you: first-time candidates do much better than those who defer.

The latest results

Level Session Pass rate Candidates
Level I Aug 2026 43% 28,145
Level I May 2026 39% 31,566
Level I Feb 2026 45% 24,006
Level I Nov 2025 43% 26,752
Level II Aug 2026 45% 10,004
Level II May 2026 43% 18,111
Level II Nov 2025 42% 15,003
Level III Aug 2026 47% 13,940
Level III Feb 2026 50% 11,269

Long-run averages

Level Five-year average (2022–2026) All-time average (1963–2026)
Level I 41% 41%
Level II 46% 46%
Level III 49% 54%

What the numbers actually say

  • Fewer than half pass at any level. Level I's 41% average means most candidates need more than one attempt somewhere in the program.
  • First-time candidates do much better. In August 2026, 49% of first-time Level I candidates passed, and 56% at Level III. Among candidates who had deferred at least once, pass rates were around 30% (for example 29% at Level I and 33% at Level III in August 2025). Sitting when you are scheduled to, prepared, is the single best predictor you control.
  • Level III is not the easiest level, even though its pass rate is the highest. The candidates who reach Level III have already passed two filters.
  • The published rate counts only people who sat. Candidates who registered and did not turn up are not in the denominator.

There is no minimum passing score published in advance. CFA Institute sets the passing standard after each exam, so aim well above the line rather than at it. A common target in prep-provider advice is 70% or more on full mock exams in the final weeks.

Questions people ask

What is the CFA Level 1 pass rate?

Recent sessions have been in the low to mid 40s percent. See the chart above for each recent session.

What is the minimum passing score?

It is not published. It is set for each exam after it is taken, so the best target is to score well above the level you hope is enough.

Why are CFA pass rates so low?

The exams are long, broad and set at a high standard, and many candidates sit while working full time. First-time candidates who study consistently do much better than the average.

Have pass rates changed over time?

They move around from session to session, but recent results are close to the long-run averages for each level.
