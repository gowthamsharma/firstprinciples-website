The CFA study planner (Excel)

Type in your level, start date, exam date and the hours you can give each day. The workbook does the rest:

  • A verdict on your plan: comfortable, tight or short, with the buffer in hours.
  • Hours per topic, weighted by how much each topic counts in the exam, with a "priority now" score that tells you what to practise next.
  • A week-by-week plan in three phases (learn, practise, mocks) with a focus topic and an hours target for every week.
  • A daily log that feeds a dashboard: hours done against plan, accuracy, mock scores.
  • A habit calendar and streak counter built on a "never zero" daily minimum, plus time-management and discipline rules that work.

Download the planner (.xlsx)

Works in Microsoft Excel, Google Sheets (File, Import) and LibreOffice. Levels I, II and III. Free, no sign-up.

What financial reporting is for

Financial statements exist to help investors and lenders decide whether to provide capital. Everything else in accounting follows from that purpose.

Financial Statement AnalysisFree lesson2 min read

How the three financial statements connect

The income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement are three views of one set of transactions. Change one, and the others must move.

Financial Statement AnalysisFree lesson2 min read

Accrual accounting: when revenue and expenses count

Revenue is recorded when it is earned and expenses when they are incurred, not when cash moves. The gaps between the two become balance sheet items.

Financial Statement AnalysisFree lesson2 min read

Revenue recognition in five steps

Revenue is recognised when a promised good or service is delivered to the customer, at the price allocated to that promise.
