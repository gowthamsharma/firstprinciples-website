CFA Level I practice questions: Quantitative methods, with worked answers

By Gowtham KumarPractice questions

Ten original quantitative methods questions in the Level I format, three options each, with an explanation for every answer. Time value of money, statistics, probability, hypothesis tests and regression. Quantitative methods carries 11–14% of the exam from 2027.

Quantitative methods carries 11–14% of Level I from 2027, as much as fixed income or equities. Most questions are either a calculation you should do in under ninety seconds on the BA II Plus, or a definition you need to state exactly (a Type I error, a p-value, what R² measures).

Topic weight from 2027: 11–14% of Level I, about 20 to 25 of the 180 questions.

10 questions · Level I format · about 15 minutes

Choose an answer to see the explanation.

  1. A deposit pays a stated annual rate of 8%, compounded quarterly. What is its effective annual rate?

    Answer: B. Quarterly compounding pays 8% / 4 = 2% each quarter, and each payment earns interest afterwards: (1.02)^4 − 1 = 8.243%. The stated rate (8.00%) ignores compounding; 8.33% is what continuous compounding would give, as if interest were added every instant.

  2. You receive 1,000 at the end of each year for five years. At a discount rate of 6%, what are the payments worth today?

    Answer: C. PV = 1,000 × [1 − 1.06^−5] / 0.06 = 4,212.36. 4,465.11 is the value if the payments came at the start of each year (an annuity due, which is the ordinary annuity × 1.06). 5,000 ignores the time value of money.

  3. A fund returned +20%, −10% and +5% in three consecutive years. What was its geometric mean annual return?

    Answer: B. Compound the growth factors and take the cube root: (1.20 × 0.90 × 1.05)^(1/3) − 1 = 1.134^(1/3) − 1 = 4.28%. 5.00% is the arithmetic mean, which overstates what an investor actually earned per year.

  4. A client adds a large sum to her account just before the portfolio performs badly. Which return measure is NOT affected by the timing of her deposit?

    Answer: A. The time-weighted return links the return of each sub-period, so it measures the manager's results regardless of when money arrives. The money-weighted return is the internal rate of return of all cash flows, so a big deposit before a bad period drags it down.

  5. Annual returns are normally distributed with a mean of 8% and a standard deviation of 12%. About how likely is a return below −16%?

    Answer: A. −16% is (−16 − 8) / 12 = 2 standard deviations below the mean. About 95% of outcomes lie within ±2 standard deviations, so about 2.5% lie in each tail; the exact one-tail figure is 2.28%. 16% would be the chance of falling more than one standard deviation below the mean.

  6. A stock returns 20% with probability 0.3, 10% with probability 0.5 and −10% with probability 0.2. What is the standard deviation of its return?

    Answer: A. Expected return = 0.3(20) + 0.5(10) + 0.2(−10) = 9%. Variance = 0.3(11²) + 0.5(1²) + 0.2(19²) = 109 (in %²). Standard deviation = √109 = 10.44%. 9% is the expected return and 109 is the variance, not the standard deviation.

  7. In a hypothesis test, what is a Type I error?

    Answer: C. A Type I error is a false alarm: the null is true but the sample led you to reject it. Its probability is the significance level. Failing to reject a false null is a Type II error.

  8. A simple regression of a fund's returns on the market's returns has a positive slope and an R² of 0.64. What is the correlation between the two return series?

    Answer: C. In a regression with one explanatory variable, R² is the square of the correlation coefficient. √0.64 = 0.80, and the positive slope tells you the sign is positive.

  9. Twenty percent of fund managers are skilled. Skilled managers beat their index in 70% of years; unskilled managers do so in 40% of years. A manager beat the index last year. What is the probability that she is skilled?

    Answer: A. By Bayes' formula: P(skilled | beat) = (0.20 × 0.70) / (0.20 × 0.70 + 0.80 × 0.40) = 0.14 / 0.46 = 30.4%. One good year raises the probability from 20%, but not by much, because unskilled managers also beat the index often.

  10. Two assets have a covariance of returns of 0.006. Their standard deviations are 10% and 20%. What is their correlation?

    Answer: C. Correlation = covariance / (σ₁ × σ₂) = 0.006 / (0.10 × 0.20) = 0.006 / 0.02 = 0.30.

Three things to remember for quantitative methods

  • Enter cash flows with signs: money you pay is negative.
  • Know which mean the question wants: arithmetic for one period ahead, geometric for what was actually earned.
  • For probability questions, write the tree before you multiply.

How to use these questions

Answer each question before you look at the explanation, and time yourself: the real exam allows about 90 seconds per question. Mark every question you got right for the wrong reason as wrong. When you miss one, write down the idea behind it rather than the answer, and come back to the page in a week.

These questions are written by First Principles to match the style and level of the exam. They are not taken from CFA Institute's curriculum or mock exams, which you should also use: they are included with your exam registration.

Questions people ask

No. CFA Institute does not publish past exam questions. These are original questions written to match the Level I format: a short stem and three answer options, testing one idea each.

Quantitative methods carries 11–14% of the exam from 2027, which is roughly 20 to 25 of the 180 questions.
