CFA Level I practice questions: Derivatives, with worked answers

By Gowtham KumarPractice questions

Ten original derivatives questions in the Level I format, three options each, with an explanation for every answer. Forwards, futures, swaps and options: pricing by no-arbitrage and payoffs. Derivatives carries 6–9% of the exam from 2027.

Derivatives at Level I is about payoffs and no-arbitrage pricing: forwards and futures, swaps, options, put-call parity and the one-period binomial model. Draw the payoff before you calculate and most questions become simple.

Topic weight from 2027: 6–9% of Level I, about 11 to 16 of the 180 questions.

10 questions · Level I format · about 15 minutes

Choose an answer to see the explanation.

  1. A stock trades at 100 and pays no dividends. The risk-free rate is 5% a year (annual compounding). What is the no-arbitrage price of a one-year forward?

    Answer: A. F = S × (1 + r)^T = 100 × 1.05 = 105. Any other price lets someone borrow, buy the stock and sell the forward (or the reverse) for a riskless profit.

  2. A stock trades at 50. A European call and put both have a strike of 50 and the same expiry. The present value of the strike is 48 and the call costs 4. What should the put cost?

    Answer: C. Put-call parity: call + PV(strike) = put + stock, so put = 4 + 48 − 50 = 2.00.

  3. You buy a call with a strike of 40 for a premium of 3. At expiry the stock is at 46. What is your profit?

    Answer: A. Payoff = 46 − 40 = 6; profit = payoff − premium = 6 − 3 = 3.

  4. What is the maximum loss for the seller of a put option with strike K who received a premium p?

    Answer: A. The put seller must buy the stock at K. The worst case is a worthless stock, a loss of K, softened by the premium received: K − p. The seller of an uncovered call is the one with unlimited loss.

  5. A call option is in the money when:

    Answer: C. A call gives the right to buy at the strike, so exercising it now has value only if the market price is higher. For a put it is the reverse.

  6. Which feature distinguishes futures from forward contracts?

    Answer: C. Futures trade on an exchange in standard sizes, and gains and losses are settled every day through a clearing house, which nearly removes counterparty risk. Forwards are private and customised.

  7. A company enters an interest rate swap in which it pays fixed and receives floating. It gains most if:

    Answer: B. Higher rates increase the floating payments it receives while its fixed payments stay the same. A pay-fixed swap behaves like issuing a fixed-rate bond and holding a floating-rate note.

  8. A stock at 100 will rise to 120 or fall to 80 in one period. The risk-free rate for the period is 5%. What is a one-period call with a strike of 100 worth?

    Answer: B. Risk-neutral probability of the up move = (1.05 − 0.80) / (1.20 − 0.80) = 0.625. The call pays 20 up and 0 down, so its value = 0.625 × 20 / 1.05 = 11.90.

  9. An investor owns a share and buys a put on it. This strategy:

    Answer: A. This is a protective put: below the strike, the put's gain offsets the share's loss, like insurance. Capping the upside for a premium describes a covered call.

  10. An investor owns a share and sells a call on it (a covered call). Compared with owning the share alone, she:

    Answer: A. The premium is extra income and a small cushion, but if the share rises above the strike it will be called away, so the upside is capped. The downside is reduced only by the premium.

Three things to remember for derivatives

  • Payoff ignores the premium; profit subtracts it.
  • Put-call parity: call + PV(strike) = put + stock.
  • In the binomial model, discount the risk-neutral expected payoff at the risk-free rate.

How to use these questions

Answer each question before you look at the explanation, and time yourself: the real exam allows about 90 seconds per question. Mark every question you got right for the wrong reason as wrong. When you miss one, write down the idea behind it rather than the answer, and come back to the page in a week.

These questions are written by First Principles to match the style and level of the exam. They are not taken from CFA Institute's curriculum or mock exams, which you should also use: they are included with your exam registration.

Questions people ask

Are these real CFA exam questions?

No. CFA Institute does not publish past exam questions. These are original questions written to match the Level I format: a short stem and three answer options, testing one idea each.

How many derivatives questions are on Level I?

Derivatives carries 6–9% of the exam from 2027, which is roughly 11 to 16 of the 180 questions.

Use the practice questions and mock exams in CFA Institute's learning ecosystem first; they come with registration. Then use one third-party question bank, and redo every question you missed.
