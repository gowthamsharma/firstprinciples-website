Building a 300-hour CFA study plan around a full-time job

Work backwards from exam day, protect the last six weeks for practice, and decide in advance what happens when a week goes wrong.

Study strategy · 2 min read

CFA Institute says successful candidates report more than 300 hours of study per level. Prep providers broadly agree, and Level II often takes more. The hours are not the hard part; consistency over five or six months is. A plan exists to make the next study session obvious, so you never spend energy deciding what to do.

Work backwards from exam day

  1. Fix the end. Put your exam date in the calendar, then block the final week as rest and light review, and the four weeks before it for full mock exams.
  2. Reserve a practice block. Before the mocks, set aside three to four weeks for topic-by-topic question practice.
  3. What is left is for learning. Divide it across the topics in proportion to their exam weights, not their page counts. For Level I in 2027, quantitative methods now deserves as much time as fixed income.

With about 22 weeks, that is roughly 13 weeks of learning, 4 of practice, 4 of mocks and 1 of rest: about 14 hours a week.

Make the weekly rhythm boring

  • Weekdays: 1 to 1.5 hours, before work if you can. Mornings are less likely to be cancelled by a late meeting.
  • Weekends: one long block (3 to 4 hours) and one shorter one.
  • One topic at a time, finishing each reading with its practice questions the same week. Reading without questions feels productive and is not.

Plan for the bad week

Something will go wrong: a deadline, an illness, a holiday. Decide now that a missed week is absorbed by the practice block, never the mock block. If you fall more than three weeks behind, cut depth in the topics with the smallest weights rather than skipping whole topics.

Track what matters

Log two numbers each week: hours studied and percentage correct on practice questions. Hours show whether the plan is realistic; accuracy shows whether it is working. Mock scores in the final month are the best predictor you have, and many providers suggest aiming for 70% or more before exam day.

The first-principles part

Most wasted hours come from re-learning things you memorised and forgot. When you meet a formula, spend five extra minutes on why it has the shape it has. The prerequisite concepts on this site are written that way.
