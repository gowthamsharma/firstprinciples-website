Profit maximisation, breakeven and shutdown

By Gowtham KumarLesson 32 of 50

A firm maximises profit where marginal revenue equals marginal cost. Whether it should keep operating at a loss depends on covering variable costs.

Produce where MR = MC. In the short run, keep going as long as price covers average variable cost.

From first principles

Why MR = MC? If one more unit brings in more than it costs (MR > MC), make it. If it costs more than it brings in (MR < MC), don't. Profit is highest where the two are equal. For a price-taker in a competitive market, MR equals the price.

When is the firm making money? If price is at least average total cost, it covers every cost: the breakeven point is price = minimum ATC.

Should a loss-making firm close? Fixed costs must be paid either way in the short run. So the right question is whether producing covers the variable costs:

  • Price above minimum AVC: producing covers variable costs and pays part of the fixed costs. Keep operating, even at a loss.
  • Price below minimum AVC: every unit loses money on top of the fixed costs. Shut down now.
  • Long run: every cost is variable, so a firm that cannot cover ATC exits.

Worked example

Fixed costs 500 per month; at the best output, AVC = 8 and ATC = 13 per unit, output 100. Price 10: revenue 1,000, variable costs 800, so operating loses 300 against 500 if closed. Keep operating. Price 7: variable costs are not covered, so shut down.

Exam traps

  • Breakeven uses ATC; shutdown uses AVC.
  • Sunk costs do not matter for the decision.

Questions people ask

Where does a firm maximise profit?

Where the revenue from one more unit equals its cost. Below that output, an extra unit adds profit; above it, an extra unit costs more than it brings in.

When should a firm shut down in the short run?

When the price no longer covers average variable cost. Fixed costs must be paid either way, so staying open makes sense as long as each sale covers its own variable cost and contributes something to fixed costs.

What is the difference between accounting and economic profit?

Economic profit also subtracts the return the owners could earn elsewhere. A firm can show an accounting profit and still destroy value if that profit is below its opportunity cost.

Perfect competition in the long run

When anyone can enter and every product is the same, profits attract rivals until prices fall to the lowest average cost. Economic profit is competed away.
